Optimisers

Two generations of one optimiser, both called Fusion, because each is a fusion of published methods. Fusion v1 (FusionOpt) is the packaged, public-domain one. Fusion v2 is its modular rebuild (ModularOptimizer in the code). It trains the project's recent control heads for Stable Audio 3.

The components are well-cited; the composition, and what we measured about it, is ours.

§ Lineage

generationwhat it is
Fusion v1 · FusionOpt
June 2026		 One optimiser that composes six published methods: Muon, NorMuon, Schedule-Free, MONA, KL-Shampoo and ScheduleFree+. You pick components from a set. Later additions: a norm freeze (hyperball), an in-optimiser decay schedule, an SNR gate, a Prodigy-style step-size autoscale and cautious masking.
Fusion v2 · modular
from September 2026		 The same ideas rebuilt as a staged pipeline. Every parameter gets a role, every role its own geometry, and every stage can be switched on or off per parameter group. The reason: in v1 it was hard to tell which piece carried a result, and an audit later found some components inert in runs that had been credited to them. v2 makes each piece separately measurable.

§ Coming from AdamW (or plain Muon)

The long version, with every change we tried, figures and audio, is Muon and derivatives: a DIY survival guide. The points that matter most when you switch:

  • Muon only replaces the optimiser for hidden 2-D matrices. Embeddings, the output layer, biases, gains and DoRA magnitudes still need a second optimiser, and how the two are set up relative to each other caused most of our failures. Print the parameter groups at start-up and read them; name-based rules miss.
  • One learning rate, three step sizes. An AdamW step moves each weight by about 0.2–0.4 × lr. An orthogonalised Muon step moves it by about lr / √max(m, n), which is 1/16 of lr for a 256-wide matrix. A sign step on biases moves it by the full lr. Rescale the Muon update to AdamW size (update RMS 0.2 × lr); after that, our heads wanted 1e-3 to 3e-3.
  • Muon never slows down by itself. AdamW's steps shrink while the gradients are noisy. Muon's step is full size every time, so weights can grow while the loss looks normal. Use weight decay on the matrices and log weight size per group: full fine-tunes without decay drifted into a drone.
  • No constant learning rate on its own. Warm up, then use Schedule-Free or a decaying schedule.
  • Keep weights and optimiser state in fp32. bf16 storage rounds small steps away; Newton–Schulz itself is fine in bf16.
  • Fine-tuning an AdamW-trained model: published work finds no clear gain from Muon for full fine-tuning, and LoRA narrows the mismatch. Small batches are mostly noise: use an effective batch of 16 or more (64 was best on our heads).

§ Fusion v2: the pipeline

Each step is a norm-constrained steepest-descent step, computed in six stages:

stagewhat it does
1 · momentumaccumulates the gradient, in the original basis or a rotated one
2 · whitening (optional)Shampoo (KL-Shampoo) or rotated-SOAP preconditioner factors, pushed on a stack (Mousse-style, with a bottleneck)
3 · solvethe role's linear minimisation oracle: spectral (Newton–Schulz orthogonalisation, cubic, quintic or Polar Express) with NorMuon per-neuron row scaling; sign; or column-norm
4 · unwhiteningpops the stack: the exact pullback into the parameters' own metric
5 · step-size control (optional)Prodigy-style escape velocity, SNR gate
6 · applylearning rate, radius scaling max(1, √(dout/din)), weight decay, Schedule-Free averaging (with a ScheduleFree+ burn-in), warmup, radial brake

Routing is a sign–spectral–sign sandwich:

  • embeddings get column-norm steps;
  • hidden attention and MLP matrices get spectral steps, with fused QKV and adaLN emitters split into their blocks first;
  • output layers, adaLN emitters, gains and biases get sign steps, or optionally an Adam group.

Added in October 2026, each default-off and bit-identical to the old step when off:

  • fp32 optimiser state for bf16 weights;
  • a NorMuon variant matching Microsoft's dion;
  • an Adam-matched update size (RMS 0.2 × lr);
  • NorMuon after unwhitening;
  • bf16 and batched Newton–Schulz;
  • multiplicative DoRA magnitude steps, so a magnitude cannot cross zero.

It also carries instruments: a half-batch gradient-noise meter, momentum spectra and direction agreement.

groups = build_modular_param_groups(model)    # role routing
optimizer = ModularOptimizer(
    groups, lr=1e-3,
    normuon=True, schedule_free=True,           # the core, as in v1's SF-NorMuon
    state_dtype="fp32",                         # keep the step when weights are bf16
)

§ Fusion v2: results

Scope: measured on one task, the LatCH onset head. That is a ~6 M-parameter transformer (d256), trained from scratch on ~12 k latent crops for 40 epochs, which guides Stable Audio 3's rhythm. Read the results as a strong prior for small models trained from scratch, and a hypothesis anywhere else.

arm (batch 64)held-out loss ↓
Fusion v2: Muon-family + Schedule-Free + EMA0.1802 (repeat run 0.1896)
AdEMAMix, continued 20 epochs from its best0.1894
AdamW, cosine to zero over 40 epochs0.2002
AdamW, constant 3e-40.2080 (swings 0.21–0.29)
AdamW, cosine + AdaGC0.2511 (stopped at epoch 12)
  • Noise first. A repeat of the same recipe moves the result by about 0.01 (≈ 5 %). The top arms tie within that; AdamW does not.
  • The mean hides where optimisers differ. Split by diffusion noise level, at low noise (t ≤ 0.45) AdamW is 25–39 % worse than Fusion v2. At high noise (t > 0.7, about half the loss) the arms differ by only 4–8 %.
  • It steers widest. The Fusion v2 head moves the generated rhythm from 2.9 to 12.1 onsets per second at guidance gain 512, the widest range of any head so far.

§ What we learned

findingwhat it means
bf16 storage eats the stepbf16 storage kept only 29 % of the fp32 update at lr 3e-4 (64 % at 1e-3), and some layers stopped moving while the loss looked fine. Keep optimiser state in fp32 when weights are bf16.
the 1-D group sets the ceilingGains and biases move a full lr per element, matrices about lr/√din. lr 3e-2 diverged until the sign group was capped at 2e-3. Give 1-D parameters their own small lr.
batch above 64Scale lr linearly, not by √batch: the momentum was signal-dominated on every matrix.
switching optimisers mid-runA fresh AdamW on a trained head moves every weight by about lr at step 1. Without warmup, held-out loss jumped from 0.180 to 0.338 and never recovered: use warmup and a low lr.
not worth it herenoise-based Muon variants (NAMO, HTMuon, the SNR gate, MONA), since no matrix was noise-dominated at our batch sizes; whitening on top of Newton–Schulz; AdaGC; stratified timesteps; larger steps along the momentum directions.

The load-bearing subset is SF-NorMuon, {ns5, normuon, sf} at bf16. Empirically it captures ~95 % of the full composition's quality lift at ~50 % of the wall-clock cost, on small-to-medium DiT-style models with adaLN-zero conditioning. Fusion v2's core is the same three pieces.

optimizer = FusionOpt(
    params=build_fusion_param_groups(model),
    lr=3e-4,
    components={"ns5", "normuon", "sf"},   # SF-NorMuon
    hot_dtype="bf16",                       # NOT fp16: NS5 overflows
)

The sharpest v1 result: FusionCC, a run that put a learned onset meter inside the training gradient, moved onset-control correlation from .584 to .880 (bootstrap CI clear of zero). It broke the sparse-rhythm floor that every other head had hit.

§ Get it

  • Fusion v1: github.com/Taikakim/fusion-optimiser. Public domain (CC0 1.0), torch >= 2.2, no other runtime dependencies. It ships docs/best_practices.md and docs/open_questions.md. An option-by-option manual covering both generations is on the docs/manual branch (draft).
  • Fusion v2: not packaged or published yet; the source lives in our private training repository.

Research code: these numbers are evidence for our workload, not a guarantee for yours.
