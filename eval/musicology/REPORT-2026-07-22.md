## Plain-language findings first

**1. The corpus is one big style with three usable substyles, not a zoo.**
Feature clustering (27 musical features) picks k=4 by silhouette (0.195 — modest, i.e.
the styles are a continuum, not islands):
- **C0 "classic full-on goa" (n=1587, 60%)** — 143 BPM, minor, dense rolling offbeat bass
  (7.6 notes/s, occupancy 0.70, 81% on the root), busy leads, most phrygian b2 usage
  (lead b2 mass 0.08). Exemplars: Green Nuns of the Revolution, Man With No Name, Transwave.
- **C1 "sparse / progressive / breakdown-heavy" (n=703, 27%)** — same tempo band but
  everything thinner: bass occupancy 0.42, drum density −0.9σ, leads slow (1.6 n/s), almost
  no b2. Exemplars: Juno Reactor, California Sunshine, Etnoscope.
- **C2 "downtempo / psydub sibling" (n=339, 13%)** — ~99 BPM (kick often off the 4-grid:
  kick_on_beat −2.5σ, i.e. broken/dub beats), minor, mid-dense bass. Exemplars: MFG (dreamy
  cuts), Bigitam/MyStic, Virtuart.
- **C3 outlier micro-cluster (n=8)** — continuous 16th-note lead streams with 161-note median
  "phrases" (Total Eclipse "Absolute Zero" live takes) — the melody literally never stops.
DBSCAN on the PCA space agrees: one dominant density blob with ~7 shoulders at moderate eps
(noise 42%), so treat substyle labels as soft tags, not hard classes.

**2. Keys: three quarters minor, tonic anywhere, C/A/D/E/F/G minor lead the pack**
(Cm 430, Am 278, Dm 207, Em 197, Fm 193, Gm 177 of 2773). BPM is tightly tri-modal at
140/135/145 with a downtempo shoulder at ~95-100.

**3. The lead-melody grammar of goa is astonishingly compact.** Two layers:
- **Layer A — the pedal.** 91% of files with a lead contain rolling same-note 16th runs
  (≥5 consecutive repeats); median 75 occurrences of the 4-repeat cell per file, p90 366.
  The single most goa thing in the corpus is *a note repeating on the 16th grid*.
- **Layer B — oscillation cells stitched into the pedal.** Collapse the repeats and the
  melodic skeleton is dominated by symmetric two-note oscillations, in descending order of
  cross-file support: **octave bounce ±12** (50% of files), **semitone trill ±1** (45%,
  typically 2↔b3 or b2↔1 — the phrygian shimmer), **whole-tone rock ±2** (42%, typically
  1↔b7), **minor-third seesaw ±3** (41%, 1↔b3 / 5↔b7), **fifth ping ±7** (33%, 1↔5 / 2↔5),
  **fourth ±5** (32%). Then stepwise zigzag turns (e.g. +1,−1,−2,+2 around b3) at ~26%.
  Directed runs exist but are secondary: short chromatic/scalar descents (b2→1, 5→b5→…)
  and ascents (1→2→…, …→b3) each in ~38-50% of files but with low within-file repetition
  (med 2-3) — they are *connective tissue*, not hooks.
- So a prototypical iconic goa lead = **minor-key 16th-note pedal on the tonic, punctuated
  every 1-2 beats by one oscillation cell (octave, semitone, tone, or m3), occasionally
  walked to a new pedal pitch by a 3-5 note scalar run**. The corpus says this with numbers,
  not vibes.

**4. What separates a "hooky" file from a meandering one (memorability correlates).**
Per file we took its best-repeated melodic contour 4-6-gram (pedals excluded) and normalized
by lead length (hook_melodic_ratio). Top decile vs bottom decile (n=259 each):
- best motif repeats **56× vs 0×** per 380 s crop (median); pedal cell 68× vs 55× (both use pedals!)
- contour vocabulary compression: **distinct-contour-gram ratio 0.41 vs 0.98** — hooky files
  re-use motif material; non-hooky files almost never state the same contour twice
- lead density **2.3 vs 0.6 notes/s**, lead note count 863 vs 214
- pitch-range discipline: **16 vs 12 semitones** (p5-p95) — hooks live in ~1.3 octaves;
  corpus-wide range median is 17, so hooky files are *not* wider, they are denser + more repetitive
- slightly MORE chromaticism (0.127 vs 0.085) — hooks tolerate color tones; blandness doesn't
- hookness is flat across eras (1990s med 0.076, 2015+ 0.079) and highest in cluster C0.
Sanity check: the top melodic-hook files are canonical: Prana "Primal Orbit" (octave bounce
×172), Planet B.E.N. "Ant Invasion" (whole-tone rock ×313), Koxbox "Insect" (×91),
Out of Orbit "Mars" (±10 = minor-7th bounce ×32).

**5. Phrasing:** median phrase is short — 1-3 notes between grid gaps (i.e. stabs and cells,
not long lines); phrase p90 within a file ~19 notes. The "melody" in goa is an accumulation
of cells over a pedal, not a sung 8-bar theme. Models that try to generate long free lines
are stylistically wrong twice over.

## Coverage and method

- 4545 stats sidecars scanned; **kept 2773 (61%)** under cutoff `integrity_flag == true AND
  integrity_pc_corr >= 0.30` (flag-only would keep 3805; corr median is 0.50 — 0.30 trims the
  noisiest transcriptions while keeping a large corpus). 1 parse failure. Of kept: 2761 have
  an inferable bass, **2649 have a lead** (skyline ≥40 notes).
- Roles: bass = non-drum track maximizing `n_notes × frac(pitch≤55)` with median pitch ≤50
  (in practice the transcriber's program-33 track); drums = `is_drum`; **lead = skyline**:
  highest onset ≥ MIDI 56 per 16th slot across remaining tracks.
- **Grid:** BPM seeded from joined `bpm_madmom`, folded to 90-200, then **beat period refined
  (±4%) by maximizing circular concentration of kick onsets** — without this, a 0.5% BPM error
  drifts the 16th grid by whole beats over 380 s (kick_on_beat went 0.27 → 0.99 after the fix).
  Phase from circular mean of kicks. Median kick_on_beat 0.89 corpus-wide.
- **Key:** Krumhansl-Schmuckler on the duration-weighted **MIDI** pitch-class profile (the
  audio `pc_profile` disagreed with MIDI note content often enough to corrupt scale-degree
  features; audio-vs-MIDI tonic agreement is recorded per file as `key_agree_audio`).
- Motifs: within-phrase n-grams (n=4-8) of successive semitone intervals (key-invariant),
  two tiers: **grid** (as played, 16th slots) and **contour** (zero-runs collapsed). Two-pass
  count→prune(≥40 global)→support/exemplars; families deduped by contained-subsequence.
- Caveat: the octave-bounce family is partly confounded by skyline switching between a lead
  and an octave doubling, and transcription octave errors; its 50% support is an upper bound.
  The ±1/±2/±3 trills are robust to this. Cluster silhouette 0.195 = soft styles. Tempo/key
  for C2 (broken-beat) files is least reliable (kick-phase grid assumes 4-on-floor).

## So-what for training (evidence-backed)

1. **Ship substyle caption tags from `clusters.json` assignments.** Three usable tags —
   `full-on goa` (C0), `sparse progressive` (C1), `downtempo psy` (C2) — cover 2629 crops with
   concrete acoustic meaning (143 BPM/dense-offbeat-bass vs thin vs 99 BPM/broken-kick). The
   current prompts (`"earth chakra, 1996, 123"`) carry no style info at all; this is free
   conditioning signal. Keep BPM in captions — the corpus is tri-modal 135/140/145 and cluster
   identity is tempo-coupled.
2. **Add motif-vocabulary caption tags for leads.** The lead grammar is 6 oscillation cells +
   pedal; the phase-3 detectors label every file today. Tags like `octave-bounce lead`,
   `semitone-trill lead`, `rolling 16th pedal lead` have 30-50% corpus support each and name
   exactly the "iconic" devices. Motif-conditioned captions are the cheapest path to making
   the model *ask-able* for memorable leads.
3. **Curate a "hooky leads" finetune/DoRA subset from `memorability.json`.** The top decile
   (259 ids, `top_decile_ids` + `per_file` for the full ranking) is quantitatively defined:
   best contour motif repeated ≥~30-56×, contour compression ≤0.5, lead ≥2 notes/s, range
   ~16 st. These files are the corpus's Prana/Koxbox-grade hook carriers. A lead-focused
   finetune weighted toward this subset attacks the "no iconic melodies" gap directly.
4. **A LatCH melody-contour head is feasible now.** `melodies.jsonl` gives 2649 aligned
   (16th-slot, pitch) lead streams — exactly the supervision a contour head needs
   (classes: rest / pedal-repeat / move±{1,2,3,5,7,12}, per 16th frame ≈ 9.5 Hz at 143 BPM,
   close to the SA3 latent frame rate). Steering on "pedal density + oscillation type" is a
   much smaller target than full melody, and the corpus shows those two knobs ARE the style.
5. **Teach repetition, not length.** Median phrase is 1-3 notes; hooky files differ from
   bland ones by 56×-repetition and 0.41 vocabulary compression, not by longer lines or wider
   range. For eval: score generated leads with `phase3_motifs.py`-style hook_melodic_ratio
   (works on any MIDI/f0 transcription) — a model that never repeats a 4-note contour twice
   (ratio ≈ 0, like our bottom decile) is measurably missing the goa hook mechanism.
