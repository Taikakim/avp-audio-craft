# Live Music Diffusion Models: Efficient Fine-Tuning and Post-Training of Interactive Diffusion Music Generators (2605.22717) — light pass

Novack, Brade, Kim, Flores García, Shikarpur, Talegaonkar, Kim, Chen, McAuley, Berg-Kirkpatrick, Huang (UCSD/MIT/Adobe), May 2026.
PDF: `2605.22717v1-Live Music Diffusion Models_ Efficient Fine-Tuning and Post-Training of Interactive Diffusion Music Generators.pdf`.
Turns an offline bidirectional block-outpainting flow-matching music diffusion model into a low-latency streaming generator via a cheap finetune, and separately fixes AR rollout error accumulation with an RL-free adversarial recipe — both pass SAO's rectified-flow and waveform-latent hard filters.

## What it contains
- **Routing + attention-mask trick for KV-caching in block-outpainting diffusion.** Standard block-AR diffusion channel-concatenates clean context with noisy target and re-encodes the whole thing with full bidirectional attention at every diffusion step — nothing is cacheable. The fix: separate input projections for clean vs. noisy frames plus a mask that forbids clean context from attending to (or being attended to by) noisy target frames. This makes the clean-context "encoding" invariant to noise level, so its K/V states can be cached. Two variants: Encoder-Decoder LMDM (caches over diffusion steps, matches Live Music Model inference complexity) and Block-Causal LMDM (also caches over time, strictly beating LMM complexity — never re-encodes old context). Obtained by finetuning an existing checkpoint in <8 GPU-hours, no architecture rebuild.
- **ARC-Forcing: RL-free adversarial rollout post-training.** Distills the flow model into a few-step generator (1-8 steps, no CFG, ping-pong sampler) by unrolling multi-block rollouts (differentiable, unlike discrete-AR sampling) and supervising the *entire rollout* — not single blocks — against a noise-aware, full-bidirectional-context discriminator trained with a relativistic objective plus an auxiliary contrastive (matching vs. shuffled caption) loss. Adapts Self-Forcing (video diffusion distribution matching) combined with the authors' own prior ARC method. Fixes gradual metric degradation over 2-minute rollouts that appears without it; reaches ~30ms/block latency, deployed via ONNX export to a C++/JUCE app on a consumer laptop, used live with three musicians (sax, guitar, cello/whale-song model) as a real-time "generative delay."
- Controls explored (text prompts + transitions, CQT/loudness sketch controls, stem-conditioned accompaniment/jamming) largely reuse existing recipes from cited prior work — CQT and loudness sketch controls from Tsai et al. 2025 and García et al. 2025, stem-conditioned jamming from Wu et al. 2025c — rather than introducing new control modalities. Prompt-transition evaluation instead follows Team et al. 2025 (the LMM paper), and getting transitions to actually work in the distilled 1-8-step, no-CFG regime required two novel inference-time additions not present in prior work: dropping the first ~180 frames of context once one prompt's weight dominates the other, and adapting the CFG++ framework to the distilled ping-pong sampler.

## What this means for us
- Passes both hard filters (rectified flow with SAO's exact z_t=(1-k)x+k*eps convention; compact learned-VAE audio latent, not mel-spectrogram) and is explicitly designed for the no-CFG few-step regime, matching the constraints on SAO's rf_denoiser checkpoints.
- Not a control-method contribution in the sense of new modalities — the sketch/stem controls are reused, and even the prompt-transition mechanism, while requiring a novel CFG++/context-dropout trick, doesn't introduce a new conditioning modality — so this doesn't touch SAO's LatCH/FusionCC/adapter research thread directly.
- The two pieces that matter are infrastructural, and both remain things SAO would have to build itself, not results SAO can just adopt: (1) the routing-mask KV-cache mechanism is a candidate blueprint for a lower-latency or streaming variant of SA3's block continuation, distinct from Longform's current SDEdit-reanchor + slerp-crossfade approach — should compose with differential attention since it operates at the mask level, not the softmax-computation level, but this is unverified and would need actual re-derivation/testing against SA3's two-softmax attention; (2) ARC-Forcing's rollout-level adversarial-relativistic-contrastive objective is a genuinely different mechanism from FusionCC's single-block t-gated consistency loss (rollout drift vs. single-step meter mismatch) and could be a candidate fix if Longform is ever found to degrade over long tracks — but no such degradation has been reported for SAO's Longform yet, so this is a speculative future tool, not a corrective to a known problem.
- Bottom line: adjacent-capability paper (streaming/interactive live-performance music AI) rather than a hit on SAO's active control-method research surface; worth keeping on file as a reference for "if we ever want real-time SA3" or "if Longform rollout stability becomes an issue," but doesn't change anything SAO should build right now.
