# Complex Time for Consciousness and Creativity in Music

**Guerino Mazzola** · read 2026-08-12 (WINTERMUTE), pp. 65–68

**Verdict: zero yield. Read so nobody else has to.**

---

## What it contains

A **philosophy-of-consciousness essay**, not music theory and not a method. Mazzola argues that
**complex time** — time with a real *and* an imaginary component, `t = t_R + i·t_I`, borrowed
from Hawking's use of imaginary time — could resolve problems about artistic consciousness and
creativity in musical performance.

The construction: a five-dimensional real vector space `ST = ℝ³ ⊕ ℂ`, splitting into physical
space-time `RST = ℝ³ ⊕ ℝ` and **"mental space-time"** `IST = ℝ³ ⊕ iℝ`, sharing the spatial part.
This is offered as a replacement for the Cartesian pineal gland — a geometry in which res
extensa and res cogitans have a shared subspace.

Motivating puzzle: a performer's rich processual activity happens "in no physical time", at a
single point of the real time line, so a "space" of consciousness must be attached to every
moment. Supporting argument is anti-reductionist (explaining mathematical thought by neuronal
activity requires mathematics, hence circular).

Honest and slightly poignant detail: he reports repeatedly emailing Hawking about complex time
and never receiving an answer, and that the theoretical physicists he consulted regard the
imaginary component as **Wick rotation — a mathematical device, not an ontology**.

## What stays ours

**Nothing.** Against Kim's three-part test this fails all three legs by the widest margin of
anything in the sweep: nothing computable from a clip, nothing askable by ear, no time series,
no quantity of any kind. It is speculative metaphysics about the nature of consciousness.

The one adjacent thread, referenced but not developed here: §5 points at Mazzola's **performance
theory** (*The Topos of Music* vol. II/III) which models performance as a transformation from
**symbolic reality (a score) to physical reality**. Symbolic→physical rendering is at least the
same *shape* as what a conditioner does. If anyone ever pursues that, it is the Topos volumes
they want, not this essay.

Recording the null plainly because a null costs a reader nothing to act on and stops the next
instance opening it. This is the cheapest kind of result to bank and the easiest to leave
unwritten.
