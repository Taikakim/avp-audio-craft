# Persistent Homology of Music Network with Three Different Distances (arXiv:2506.13595, Journal of Mathematics and Music, submitted) — Heo, Choi, Kim, Tran, Jung (POSTECH/KIAS)

**Relevance to SAO: off-lane — pure symbolic-music TDA methodology paper, no audio, no ML, no generative or control mechanism. Conceptual cross-reference at best.**
**Status: DEEP-READ (full 23 pages incl. appendix tables, 2026-08-12, GHOST-NOTE).**

This is a math paper, not an audio/ML paper. It builds a **monophonic** note-transition graph from symbolic score data — each node is a `(pitch, duration)` tuple, an edge connects two nodes that occur consecutively in the piece, and edge weight is the **reciprocal of co-occurrence frequency** (more frequent transitions = "closer"). The contribution is entirely about how to turn that weighted graph into a metric space for persistent homology (Vietoris-Rips filtration), since a graph with weighted edges doesn't have a unique node-to-node distance unless every pair is directly connected.

The paper defines and compares three path-based distance functions for non-adjacent nodes:
- **d1** — sum of edge weights along the path with the **fewest edges** (ties broken by a fixed vertex ordering via unweighted Dijkstra).
- **d2** — sum of edge weights along the path that **minimizes total weight** (standard weighted Dijkstra / shortest path).
- **d3** — the minimum total weight **among only the paths that also have the minimum edge count** (i.e., d1's path set, but weight-minimized within it) — explicitly framed as the hybrid of d1 and d2.

Formal results (with proofs): only **d2 is a true metric** (satisfies the triangle inequality); d1 and d3 both fail it, demonstrated with an explicit 3-node counterexample (Fig. 8: `d1(b,c)=10 > d1(a,b)+d1(a,c)=2`). d1 is also **order-dependent** — different vertex orderings of the underlying Dijkstra tie-break give different path choices and thus different distances for the same graph (Example 3.13); d2 and d3 are provably order-independent. A universal inequality is proved: **d2(v,w) ≤ d3(v,w) ≤ d1(v,w)** for every pair of nodes, for any music graph. The main theorem (4.3) then lifts this into the persistent-homology domain: for the 1-dimensional persistence barcodes built from each distance, there exist **injections φ2,3 and φ3,1** mapping barcode elements d2→d3→d1, giving an **inclusion relation on birth-edge sets B2 ⊆ B3 ⊆ B1** — i.e., every topological cycle detected under d2 is also detected (with a longer or equal-length persistence interval) under d3, and every d3 cycle is also detected under d1. Practically this means d1 (edge-count-based) surfaces the most cycles / most sensitive structure, d2 (weight-based, the actual metric) the fewest/smoothest, d3 in between.

Empirical validation: 12 Korean traditional court-music pieces (geomungo and haegeum instrumental parts from *Junggwangjigok*/*Sangnyeongsan* etc.) plus 4 variations of "Twinkle Twinkle Little Star," all **monophonic, symbolic (transcribed score) note sequences** — not audio. Example concrete numbers given for *Sanghyeondodeuri* (geomungo part): **7 H1 barcodes under d1, 4 under d3, 3 under d2** — matching the predicted inclusion. Full birth/death/cycle tables for all 16 pieces are dumped in an appendix (Tables 1, 2a–2c). No downstream task is attempted (no generation, no classification, no retrieval) — the entire empirical section is "does the proven inclusion relation hold on real note-transition graphs," and it does, exactly.

**What it does NOT contain:** no audio signal or continuous-latent analysis of any kind — everything operates on hand-transcribed, monophonic, symbolic `(pitch,duration)` sequences. No connection to VAE/DiT latents, chroma features, onset density, or any of SAO's actual control targets. No generative modeling, no steering/control mechanism, no evaluation metric proposed for generated audio. No polyphony (the authors explicitly flag this as future work, citing Gomez et al. [8] for a possible pitch-set/scale-projection extension) — directly irrelevant to a full-mix EDM/goa corpus. No performance numbers comparable to anything SAO measures (no correlation/authority/disentanglement metrics — this is a pure existence-and-inclusion proof, validated by "the counts match the theorem," not by any downstream quality signal). The paper's own conclusion states the practical payoff is speculative future work: "explore the musical implications... within localized subgraphs" and "investigate automatic music generation based on varying distance functions" — nothing has been built yet even within the authors' own symbolic-music domain, let alone audio.

The closest thing to a takeaway for this project is conceptual, not methodological: the general idea that persistent homology over a well-chosen distance can find repeated/cyclic structure in a sequence is the same intuition behind wanting a structural/repetition-coherence metric for long-form generated music (relevant to SAO's long-form/loop-detection work, e.g. `stable_audio_3/inference/longform.py` and the E1 anti-loop RecurrenceHead work per WORKLOG). But this paper offers no transferable machinery for that — it would require building an analogous note/event graph from a symbolic transcription of generated audio (e.g. via a MIDI/beat/onset extraction pass) from scratch; nothing here operates on continuous embeddings or audio directly. Companion papers cited within (Tran et al. [13],[14] — "Machine composition of Korean music via TDA and ANN," "cycle structure in Jeongganbo") are the same group's closer attempts at using this cycle structure for generation, and would be the more relevant follow-up reads if this direction is ever pursued, but they are still fully symbolic/monophonic Korean-court-music-specific work, not audio-domain.
