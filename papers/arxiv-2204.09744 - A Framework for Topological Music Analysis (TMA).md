# A framework for topological music analysis (TMA) (arXiv:2204.09744, Journal of Mathematics and Music) — Alberto Alcalá-Álvarez, Pablo Padilla-Longoria

**Relevance to SAO: off-lane conceptual cross-reference — symbolic-score (MIDI/MusicXML) musicology tool, not audio; no method, model, or metric here is directly portable to a latent-diffusion audio system without building an entire symbolic-transcription front end first.**
**Status: DEEP-READ (full PDF, 2026-08-12, GHOST-NOTE).**

The paper's actual contribution is narrow and descriptive, not generative or predictive: given a digital score (parsed via `music21` from MIDI/.xml/.mxl), it defines **six different ways ("data mappings" I–VI) to embed a sequence of chords (vertical events) as points in a Euclidean space** ℝⁿ, then runs standard TDA (Vietoris–Rips filtration → persistent homology → barcodes/persistence diagrams, computed via Ripser/Persim/Scikit-TDA) on each embedding, and compares fragments/pieces via the **bottleneck distance** between their diagrams, visualized as dendrograms. The six mappings are genuinely distinct constructions, not variants of one idea:
- **I/II**: chord-as-vector (normal-form pitch classes, mapping I; or interval-vector, mapping II) concatenated with duration+onset (temporal, so NOT transposition-invariant).
- **III**: interval-vector only (transposition-invariant, coarsest — "less homological features").
- **IV**: binary pitch-class indicator vector in {0,1}¹² ⊂ ℝ¹² (a chord's presence/absence per pitch class; two chords are close in this embedding iff `‖a-b‖=√k` for k differing pitch classes).
- **V**: normal-form pitch classes only (projection of I dropping time; transposition-*sensitive* — the only other mapping besides I that detects transposition).
- **VI**: pitch class + the interval to the *next* pitch class in normal form (captures voicing/interval structure, not just set content).

Separately (§1.3, independent of any metric/embedding), the paper defines **two families of simplicial complexes built directly from chord content**: (a) "cumulative chords by pitch" — each n-chord becomes an (n−1)-simplex on its pitch-class vertices, and consecutive chords' simplices are unioned/faced together into a growing complex K(i,j) over an interval of the score, from which Betti numbers β₀…β₁₁ are computed (dimension capped at 11 since there are only 12 pitch classes); and (b) the same idea but simplices are built from *consecutive* pitch-class pairs in the chord's interval structure (captures voice-leading-adjacent interval info that (a) discards). These are NOT filtrations of a metric point cloud — they're "shape" descriptors of literal harmonic overlap between chords, with a companion **local/windowed variant** K_r(i) (radius-r neighborhood around each event) that IS a genuine filtration and does yield real persistent-homology barcodes (Fig. 12).

Empirical content is a single small worked comparison (§2), not a benchmark: the first 4 measures of 4 pieces — J.S. Bach's *Brandenburg Concertos* 1–3 and two of Armando Luna's *Graffiti* miniatures (modern homages built on letter-to-note ciphers of composer names) — are compared pairwise via bottleneck distance across all 6 mappings, reported as a distance table (Table 1, e.g. Bach BC1 vs BC2 = 0.5 under mapping IV, vs 8.72 under mapping I) and dendrograms (Figs. 15–25). The qualitative finding: Brandenburg 1 and 2 (both in F major) cluster closest under the temporally-aware, transposition-sensitive mappings (I, V), consistent with shared tonality, while the interval-only mappings (II, III) instead group by *harmonic/voicing style* rather than key. Luna's piece consistently comes out as the outlier across mappings — attributed qualitatively to its more chromatic/non-diatonic auxiliary-note content. No classification accuracy, no cross-validation, no statistical test, no comparison against a non-topological baseline (e.g. plain PCP/chroma-histogram distance) is reported — the authors explicitly frame this as a first proof-of-concept over "100+ fragments from 13 composers" (footnote 5) with only the 4-piece comparison written up in detail, plus a handful of illustrative single-fragment barcode plots (Brubeck's "My Favorite Things", a Haydn symphony, a Raga Asavari phrase — Figs. 30–34, shown but not analyzed quantitatively).

**What it does NOT contain:**
- **No audio.** Everything operates on symbolic score events (pitch class + onset + duration parsed from a digital score file). There is no signal-processing front end, no spectral/latent representation, and no discussion of how one would go from a waveform (let alone a SAME latent) to the chord/vertical-event abstraction this method needs. Porting this to SAO would require building (or reusing) a full chord/vertical-event transcription pipeline first — the paper assumes it as an input, doesn't build it.
- **No generative or steering mechanism.** This is a descriptive/comparative analysis tool (style classification via clustering, per the stated future-work goal), not a control method, loss, or conditioning signal. There is no proposal to use these topological features as a training target, a guidance signal, or a differentiable loss — the authors' own "future work" says they intend to explore ML use (classification) *later*, citing others' work (Deng & Duzhin 2022; Park, Hwang & Yang 2022) as precedent, not doing it themselves here.
- **No quantitative validation of the method's usefulness** — no benchmark task, no accuracy/F1/classification numbers, no ablation showing the topological features add value over a simpler baseline (e.g. plain pitch-class-set Hamming/Jaccard distance, which several of their own embeddings — IV especially — essentially reduce to before the TDA machinery is applied). The "results" are hand-interpreted dendrogram clusterings on 4 short excerpts, explicitly framed as illustrative, not a validated method.
- **No handling of expressive/continuous features** the SAO team actually cares about (timbre, dynamics, articulation beyond duration, onset density as a continuous rate, energy). The framework is pitch/interval/rhythm-onset only, in 12-tone equal temperament (they note it *could* extend to microtonal/continuous settings but do not do so here).
- **Compute cost is non-trivial and not discussed for anything beyond toy fragments** — a footnote admits H₁+ persistent homology "overflow[ed]" on ~500-event (32-bar) samples and Ripser's own docs are quoted noting H₁ is slow in practice; nothing here suggests this is viable as a per-step or online signal at generation time.
- **Voice-leading / chord-to-chord connection structure is explicitly left out** — the authors state this is exactly what they plan to add in a *follow-up* paper (two more simplicial-complex families for "chord connections"), so even within its own symbolic-score lane this paper is admittedly incomplete.

Net: a methodologically clean but empirically thin exploratory paper in **pure musicology / symbolic-MIR** territory. Its main transferable idea — bottleneck-distance-between-persistence-diagrams as a style/similarity descriptor — is a reasonable one to file next to the chord-as-simplex prior art it cites (Bigo, Giavitto & Spicher 2011; Bigo et al. 2013; Bergomi 2015; Bergomi & Baratè 2020) for the "topological features as eval/style metrics" thread, but nothing here is closer to actionable for SAO's chroma/onset-density/energy LatCH control heads or for an audio-domain evaluation metric than the raw chroma/onset tooling already in the repo (`stable_audio_3/inference/chroma_losses.py`, `sa3_control`'s onset-density evaluators). If the topology-of-music reading sweep surfaces a paper that does persistent homology **on audio features or embeddings directly** (not a parsed score), that would be the more load-bearing find for this project.
