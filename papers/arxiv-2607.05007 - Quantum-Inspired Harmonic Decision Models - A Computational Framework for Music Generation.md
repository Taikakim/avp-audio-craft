# Quantum-Inspired Harmonic Decision Models: A Computational Framework for Music Generation (arXiv:2607.05007) — Pavlíček, Pavlíčková, Molhanec (CTU Prague)

**Relevance to SAO: off-lane — symbolic lead-sheet chord harmonization, no audio/latent/topology content of any kind; not actionable.**
**Status: DEEP-READ (full PDF, 17 pages, 2026-08-12, GHOST-NOTE).**

The task is melody→chord-symbol harmonization for lead sheets (MusicXML in/out), demonstrated on two tunes: "Autumn Leaves" and "It's a Long Way to Tipperary." The pipeline is two classical stages wrapped in quantum vocabulary:

1. **"Quantum-inspired" harmonizer (§4).** At each beat/time-step `t`, instead of picking one chord deterministically, the model keeps a *weighted set* of chord candidates, written as `ψ_t = Σ_{c∈C_t} w(c)·|c⟩`. The paper is explicit that these weights "do not need to satisfy strict normalization constraints" — i.e. this is not a probability amplitude, not a unit vector in a Hilbert space, just a scored candidate list (a beam/particle-set). Candidates are scored on melodic compatibility, functional (T/S/D) coherence, and voice-leading smoothness via an unspecified scalar function `E(c_1,...,c_T)` (never given a concrete formula anywhere in the paper), and weights are iteratively reinforced/suppressed — called "interference" but is just standard iterative reweighting of a heuristic objective. No update equation for `w(c)` is given either; Figure 1 is a prose flowchart, not an algorithm.
2. **Classical refinement (§5).** A rule-based post-processor does harmonic smoothing (replace/insert/resolve unstable chords), inversion selection (minimize voice displacement), and cadence stabilization (enforce dominant→tonic at phrase ends). This is entirely conventional tonal-harmony rule-following — no quantum framing attached to this stage at all.

**Empirical results (§6, both metrics and 2 professional + 6 non-expert listener ratings, on 2 songs):** the classical-refinement stage reduces chord density (Autumn Leaves 4.00→2.71 changes/measure, Tipperary 4.00→2.53), roughly doubles average chord duration (1.00→1.87 and 1.00→1.98), shifts the seventh-chord ratio down (~100%→77.6%/74.4%), and rebalances tonic/dominant function usage (e.g. Tipperary dominant share 14.7%→23.5%). Subjectively, the refined output was rated *more* natural for the harmonically-rich Autumn Leaves but *less* natural for the simpler, diatonic Tipperary (increased complexity read as over-elaboration on a simple tune) — the authors report this divergence honestly rather than cherry-picking the favorable case.

**Is the "quantum" framing doing real mathematical work? No — and the authors say so themselves.** Section 4.4 states outright: "the present model remains entirely classical in its implementation. The reference to quantum-inspired mechanisms serves primarily as a conceptual framework." There is no Hilbert space, no complex amplitudes, no unitary evolution operator, no measurement/collapse rule, no entanglement, and — despite invoking QAOA by name in the intro — no quantum circuit, no variational ansatz, no qubits, not even a classical QAOA simulation. "Superposition" = a weighted list; "interference" = score-based reweighting of that list. This is honest, low-key branding rather than fabricated rigor (a genuine positive relative to hype-driven "quantum music AI" papers), but it means the quantum vocabulary contributes zero testable mathematical content beyond what a plain weighted beam-search harmonizer already has.

**What it does NOT contain:** no audio generation or audio representation of any kind (input/output is symbolic melody + chord-symbol MusicXML, not waveforms or latents); no deep learning / neural network anywhere in the pipeline (it's a classical constraint-search + rule engine); no topology, persistent homology, simplicial complexes, or any geometric-music-theory formalism — despite being read as part of a topology/geometry-of-music literature sweep, this paper has nothing to do with that subfield, it only shares the generic "novel mathematical framing applied to music" positioning; no concrete algorithm/pseudocode for the core scoring function `E` or the weight-update rule (both are described only in prose); no comparison against any baseline harmonization system (Markov chain, HMM, rule-based, or otherwise); evaluation is 2 songs + 8 listeners, explicitly flagged by the authors as too small for statistically robust conclusions, plus a noted bias risk since both test tunes are famous jazz/folk standards listeners already know. Code/data claimed public at `github.com/JosefPavlicek/quantum-harmonic-decision-model` (not verified here).

Not a candidate for control-method, feature-extraction, or eval-metric ideas for SA3/LatCH — wrong modality (symbolic chords, not continuous audio latents) and the "quantum" mechanism has no exportable math (it's literally a weighted-candidate reranker). No further cross-reference value expected from this paper's own bibliography (standard quantum-cognition citations — Khrennikov, Busemeyer & Bruza — already the usual suspects in that adjacent literature, not audio-ML).
