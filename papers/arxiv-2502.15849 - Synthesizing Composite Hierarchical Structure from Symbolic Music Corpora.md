# Synthesizing Composite Hierarchical Structure from Symbolic Music Corpora (arXiv:2502.15849v4, cs.AI) — Shapiro, Huang, Novack, Wang, Dong, Berg-Kirkpatrick, Dubnov, Lerner (UCSD / AudioShake)

**Relevance to SAO: off-lane — a symbolic-MIDI corpus-analysis/musicology tool (graph edit distance + SMT solving), not a generative model and not topological (no persistent homology). Conceptual cross-reference at best.**
**Status: DEEP-READ (full PDF, 2026-08-12, GHOST-NOTE).**

First, a framing correction for the reading sweep: despite the sweep's "topology/geometry-of-music" framing, this paper contains **no persistent homology, simplicial complexes, or TDA of any kind**. It is a classical graph-theory/combinatorial-optimization paper — graph edit distance (GED), simulated annealing (SA), and SMT solving (Z3) over discrete symbolic-music graphs. If the team wants TDA specifically, this is not that paper; it's adjacent only in the loose sense of "structure of music as a mathematical object."

**What the paper actually does:**
- Defines a **structural temporal graph (STG)**: a k-partite DAG where each of the k layers is one level of a music-theoretic structural hierarchy — top to bottom: contiguous segmentation → motifs (disjoint + overlapping patterns) → relative keys → functional harmonic chords → melodic contour. Edges encode both parent/child structural containment (a node's time interval is a subset of, or starts/ends within, its parent's) and, within a level, a linear temporal chain between adjacent nodes. Each level's labels are generated automatically by an off-the-shelf symbolic-MIR analyzer: flat Structural Features (segmentation, Nieto & Bello 2014), BPS-motif discovery (Hsiao et al. 2023), Harmony Transformer V2 (keys/chords, Chen & Su 2021), Melodia (melodic contour, Salamon et al. 2014). The STG is thus only as good as these component analyzers — the authors say this outright.
- **Structural distance**: to compare two STGs they need graph isomorphism-aware alignment (raw GED treats node identity as fixed, which doesn't work since node ids encode features + intra-level order). They pad adjacency matrices to equal size with dummy nodes, then use simulated annealing to find the permutation matrix P minimizing ||A_G1 − PᵀA_G2P||_F (Eq. 1–2); this equals √GED under optimal alignment. The SA move function swaps two node indices within the same partition (level or feature-prototype group) so moves respect the graph's k-partite structure.
- **Centroid derivation**: formalized as a constrained/nested instance of the NP-hard Generalized Median Graph problem (find g minimizing Σ d(g,G) over a corpus). Solved via **bi-level nested simulated annealing** (outer anneals which edges to flip in the candidate centroid, scored by total aligned loss against the corpus; inner is the alignment annealer from above, re-run at every outer step) followed by an **SMT repair step using Z3** that projects the annealed-but-structurally-invalid approximate centroid onto the nearest graph satisfying the STG's well-formedness constraints (global constraints like no self-loops/no cross-level-skipping edges; per-level and per-prototype constraints in Tables 1–3), since the constraints are formalized as quantifier-free first-order logic and Z3's optimizer minimizes deviation from the approximate centroid (Eq. 6) subject to those constraints.

**Empirical results (real numbers, cite-checked against the PDF):**
- *Structural-distance validation*: on 210 pairwise distances across 5 five-composer sets (32 MIDI pieces: Bach, Mozart, Beethoven, Schubert, Chopin), a Mantel test against ground-truth composer-similarity indices (from "The Classical Music Navigator," Smith & Georges 2014) gives Spearman ρ_s = **0.8207** (p=0.0130) for their structural distance — beating a MIDI-feature cosine-similarity baseline (ρ_s=0.4681, p=0.315, not significant) and an audio-similarity baseline SWAS (ρ_s=0.5775, p=0.169, not significant). Notably, a Weisfeiler-Lehman graph kernel baseline gets ρ_s=**−0.8389** (p=0.011) — significant but in the *wrong direction*, i.e. actively anti-correlated with ground truth.
- *Level-ablation*: progressively stripping levels off the STG monotonically degrades the correlation: 5 levels 0.8207 → 4 levels 0.7842 → 3 levels 0.7173 → 2 levels 0.6930 → 1 level **−0.4377** (sign flip). This is their evidence that the *full* hierarchy, not any single level, is what carries the signal.
- *Centroid validation, synthetic*: given a base STG g with |E| edges, they build synthetic corpora by randomly applying valid edits to g (so g is provably the true centroid by a triangle-inequality argument, proved in Appendix D). Across corpus sizes k∈[3,14], the derived centroid's relative loss error vs. the true centroid maxes at 2.99% (k=5) and hits 0% at k=10,11 — and beats a naive "closest existing corpus member" baseline by ~17.23% on average.
- *Centroid validation, real corpora*: on four composer corpora (Alkan 11, Chopin 8, Haydn 12, Mozart 14 pieces, ≤80s each), they mine all common 5-node subgraphs across each corpus and check how many also appear in the derived centroid: 76.67% (Alkan), 70.06% (Chopin), 63.90% (Haydn), 70.68% (Mozart) — presented as evidence the centroid captures a corpus's structurally salient shared substructure (e.g. the Mozart example centroid subgraph is "two consecutive major chords in the same major key, forming a motif").

**Caveats the authors themselves note:** GED/alignment and centroid derivation are both NP-hard, hence the SA+SMT machinery rather than exact solution; approximate annealed centroids can be "locally invalid" and require the Z3 repair pass; centroids are not unique (multiple non-isomorphic graphs can tie at minimal SOD, "considered equivalent as centroids by definition"); the whole pipeline is only as accurate as the four component analyzers feeding each level (they explicitly flag generated analyses as "slightly inaccurate," e.g. a chord mislabeled to the wrong beat in their walkthrough figure); larger-than-5-node subgraph mining was intractable, and approximate tools were "too imprecise for meaningful conclusions." Runtime: alignment annealing used 2000 iterations on 8× RTX 2080 (11GB) GPUs; centroid annealing used 24 Intel Xeon cores / 32GB RAM — this is corpus-analysis-scale compute, not per-sample/real-time.

**What it does NOT contain (the "what remains ours" section):**
- No generative model. The paper is explicit that "structured, controllable sequence generation" is **future work** ("users could modify the STG of a generated music piece to update constraints on a generative model") — nothing here actually conditions or steers a generator. There is zero connection to diffusion, flow-matching, or any neural generative architecture.
- No audio. Everything operates on symbolic MIDI (converted from MIDI→CSV for analysis, MIDI→MP3 via Fluidsynth only for the SWAS baseline's audio-similarity comparison). All four component analyzers used to build STG levels (segmentation, motif discovery, key/chord via Harmony Transformer V2, melody via Melodia) are symbolic- or monophonic-melody-extraction tools operating on discrete note/onset/pitch events — none of them are the kind of thing that runs on a continuous learned VAE latent like SAME's. Porting the STG idea to SAO's raw-audio-latent setting would require first solving open, much-harder problems (robust motif discovery and key/chord estimation from polyphonic audio mixes, not scores) — the paper doesn't attempt or discuss this gap at all.
- No topology/TDA (as flagged above) — no persistent homology, simplicial complexes, Betti numbers, filtrations, or anything in that family. Purely classical graph theory (GED, k-partite DAGs) + combinatorial optimization (SA, SMT/Z3).
- No differentiability. GED-via-annealing and Z3-repair are discrete, iterative, non-gradient procedures — nothing here is a drop-in loss term or backprop-compatible metric for a training loop.
- Test corpora are all solo/small classical piano pieces (Bach, Mozart, Beethoven, Schubert, Chopin, Alkan, Haydn), each piece length-matched to ≤80s or within 7s of siblings in a set — no polyphonic multi-instrument or electronic/dance material anything like SAO's Goa/trance corpus, and no claim of generalization beyond this regime is made.

**Bottom line for SAO:** the STG-as-corpus-summary idea is a mildly interesting general concept — a k-partite hierarchy graph + a distance/centroid over it — but every piece of machinery here (the four symbolic analyzers, NP-hard SA/SMT solving, MIDI-only evaluation) is specific to symbolic corpora and non-differentiable, non-real-time musicology tooling. It does not offer a usable control mechanism, feature-extraction method, or evaluation metric for SAO's raw-audio rectified-flow DiT / LatCH setup without first building an entirely separate (and currently unsolved) audio→symbolic-hierarchy extraction pipeline — which the paper neither provides nor discusses. Recommend filing as background/off-lane rather than actionable.

The **structural-coherence-to-corpus-centroid score** (graph-edit-distance against the derived centroid, ρ_s=0.8207 vs ground-truth composer similarity) is a real candidate under that lens — a single number for "how structurally typical/coherent is this piece for its style," plausibly something Kim would ask for by ear ("more structured," "more coherent," "more like [reference style]"). Also genuinely a whole-piece scalar as defined, not naturally time-resolved like the transition-matrix/height-function papers elsewhere in this batch; windowing it would need re-deriving the GED formulation over sub-spans, not just narrowing the input.
