# Diffusion Domain Expansion: Learning to Coordinate Pre-Trained Diffusion Models (2605.23275) — light pass

Lifar, Savkin, Garipov, Tong, Jaakkola (MIT CSAIL). ICML 2024 SPIGM workshop paper, arXiv May 2026.
PDF: `2605.23275v1-Diffusion Domain Expansion- Learning to Coordinate Pre-trained Diffusion Models.pdf`.
A trained (not training-free) alternative to MultiDiffusion-style patch-stitching for extending a pretrained diffusion model to longer sequences or more conditioning than it was trained on — directly adjacent to SAO's shipped Longform pipeline, but via a different mechanism class.

## What it contains
- Problem: given a pretrained conditional diffusion model `p(x|y)` that only knows how to generate objects/conditioning of size `L_train`, generate coherent objects/conditioning of size `L_test >> L_train` by decomposing the large object into overlapping smaller pieces, running the frozen pretrained denoiser on each piece independently, then **reconciling** the per-piece denoised (predicted-clean) outputs into one coherent whole.
- Baselines for reconciliation are naive concatenation and MultiDiffusion-style overlap-averaging of per-patch outputs (concatenation is used as a baseline in the music experiment only; CLEVR is compared against RRR and MultiDiffusion, satellite tiling against MultiDiffusion only). DDE's contribution: replace the averaging step with a small trained "coordinator" network — a ViT (RoPE-based 2D/1D positional encoding for length generalization) — that takes all patches' pretrained-denoiser outputs plus their positions/conditioning and predicts an improved, jointly coherent set of outputs; overlaps are still reconciled by simple averaging at the ViT output stage.
- Trained on short examples (e.g. `L_train=5` patches / 48s music) and evaluated zero-shot at much larger sizes (`L_test=13` patches / 120s music; CLEVR conditioning generalized from ≤2 objects seen in training to 5 at test; satellite-image tiling from 96×96 train to 128×128 test). Coordinator training always converges in far fewer epochs than the base model (10 vs. ~80–300 epochs music; 20 vs. 200 map; 20 vs. 1050 CLEVR), but parameter-count savings are domain-dependent: much smaller for music (66M vs. 405M) and map (26M vs. 270M), but only marginally smaller for CLEVR (38M vs. 42M — essentially comparable size, not "tens vs. hundreds of millions").
- Results: DDE beats concatenation and MultiDiffusion baselines on FAD (long music, Slakh2100/UNet1d base model), and beats MultiDiffusion/RRR on classifier-verified accuracy (CLEVR multi-object conditioning, especially at 4-5 conditions) and FID (satellite image outpainting), and the size/conditioning-count generalization is empirically demonstrated to hold well beyond training range.
- Base models throughout are Karras/EDM-style score-based diffusion (raw-waveform UNet1d for music at 22.05kHz, UNet-based for images) — classic DDPM/score-SDE formalism, not rectified flow; music operates directly on raw time-domain audio samples, not a learned latent (neither mel nor a waveform-VAE bottleneck).

## What this means for us
- **Fails both hard filters as shipped**: (1) DDPM/EDM ancestral+Heun-sampler framework, not rectified-flow — would need re-expression of "coordinator operates on denoiser's clean-sample output" via SA3's `z0|t = z_t - t*v` predicted-clean-latent, which the differential-attention/AdaLN/DiT wrapper adds further complication to. (2) music base model diffuses directly on raw 22.05kHz waveform samples via UNet1d, not through any latent — even further from SAME's 256-dim/10.77Hz bottleneck than the mel-based lineage the filter already flags. Nothing here is drop-in.
- **The abstraction that *does* transfer**: a small trained coordinator operating on **predicted-clean estimates of overlapping patches** (rather than raw noisy latents or attention maps) to reconcile them into a longer/larger coherent whole, using RoPE for train-short/generalize-long positional extrapolation. This maps cleanly onto SA3's `z0_hat` (which SAO already computes for FusionCC's t-gated consistency loss) and onto SA3's ~10.77Hz frame grid, so the re-derivation is plausible, not hand-wavy.
- **Directly competes with, not duplicates, SAO's shipped Longform.** Longform (`stable_audio_3/inference/longform.py`) already solves "generate longer than trained" via a training-free SDEdit re-anchor + latent slerp crossfade + inpaint-continuation pipeline. DDE's pitch is that a small *trained* coordinator beats naive overlap-averaging (its closest baseline, "MultiDiffusion") by a wide margin on FAD — implying training-free overlap/crossfade schemes may be leaving quality on the table relative to a learned reconciler, at the cost of needing extra training data and a small extra network. This is a legitimate quality-vs-simplicity tradeoff argument SAO should be aware of, but it is not evidence that Longform is broken — no head-to-head against SDEdit-crossfade-style methods is in this paper, and the base models tested are far weaker/smaller than SA3.
- **CLEVR multi-conditioning-count generalization angle** (compose N frozen single-condition models to satisfy N conditions never jointly seen at training) is a cute idea but doesn't obviously apply to SA3: SA3's local-additive pathway already accepts arbitrary per-frame conditioning (chroma/onset) natively without needing to compose separate single-condition model instances, so this half of the paper has no clear import for SAO.
- **Net verdict**: interesting as a pointer to "a lightweight trained reconciler on z0_hat, not raw denoising, might beat our training-free crossfade for long-form quality" — worth a line in the Longform/frontier notes as a possible future ablation (train a small DiT/ViT coordinator on SA3 z0_hat patches, RoPE over the 10.77Hz frame axis) — but it is workshop-paper-scale evidence (small UNet1d raw-audio base models, no rectified-flow/DiT/SAME-latent result anywhere) and requires nontrivial re-derivation before it says anything directly about SA3. Not urgent, not a gap that changes current plans.
