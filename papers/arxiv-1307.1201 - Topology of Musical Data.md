# Topology of Musical Data (arXiv:1307.1201, Journal of Mathematics and Music 2013) — William A. Sethares, Ryan Budney

**Relevance to SAO: conceptual cross-reference only — a 2013 music-theory/topology methods paper on symbolic (MIDI note/chord) data; no audio, no learned model, no generative or control mechanism. Not directly actionable for SA3 control-head design, but useful as a citable prior for "circle/torus structure is the ground truth in pitch/rhythm space," which is a sanity check for chroma/circular feature representations.**
**Status: DEEP-READ (full PDF, 33 pp incl. appendices, 2026-08-12, GHOST-NOTE).**

The paper has two independent halves, both operating on **symbolic note-level MIDI data**, never audio:

1. **Classical topology of "the space of all possible musical data."** The authors define several metrics on musical objects and show each induces a specific quotient-space topology:
   - *Necklace distance* `d_N(f,g) = min(s, 1-s)`, `s = |f-g| mod 1` — circular distance between two rhythmic event-times in one period; the underlying space is literally `S¹ = ℝ/ℤ`.
   - *Pitch-class distance* `d_PC(f,g) = min(s,1-s)`, `s = |log₂f - log₂g| mod 1` — same circular construction but on log-frequency mod octave; also `S¹`.
   - *Chord-class distance* `d_CC(f,g) = min_σ Σ d_PC(f_i, σ(g_i))` — permutation-minimized sum over pitch-class distances, i.e. an **optimal-transport/Hungarian-style matching** between two n-note chords, invariant to voicing/inversion order. Its quotient space is the **symmetric product** `Symm_n(S¹) = (S¹)ⁿ/Σₙ`.
   - *Rhythm distance* — same idea but with cyclic-shift invariance instead of full permutation invariance, landing in a **sub-symmetric product** `Symm_n^G(S¹)` for `G` a subgroup of `Σₙ` (e.g. the cyclic group for circular-shift-equivalent rhythm patterns).
   - They then work out, via a theorem of Morton (1967) on `Symm_n(S¹)` as a fibre bundle over `S¹` with fibre a disc `D^{n-1}` (orientable iff n is odd), the *explicit* homeomorphism type of several of these spaces for small n: e.g. `Symm_2(S¹)` = Möbius band, `Symm_3(S¹)` with cyclic-melody symmetry `≅ S¹ × S²`, the 4-note chord-class space `Symm_4(S¹)` = the non-orientable orbifold Callender/Quinn/Tymoczko's voice-leading geometry (2008 Science paper) — this paper shows their metric coincides with `d_CC` and derives it from scratch via Morton's bundle theorem rather than ad hoc construction. This half is pure math (bundle theory, group actions, Poincaré polynomials via Künneth) — no data-driven step at all.

2. **Persistent homology on real note/chord/rhythm data**, using the standard Vietoris-Rips construction (`javaPlex` software) over the distance matrices from part 1. They feed in note-time sets (King's "standard rhythm pattern," Ewe/Yoruba/Bemba variants — all circular shifts of each other, hence identical barcodes), C-major scale (8 pitch-class points), a folk tune "Abbott's Bromley Horn Dance" (MIDI from a public tune archive), a Turkish makam (Ussak, non-equal-tempered), the circle of fifths (12 major-scale point-clouds under chord-class distance), Bach Chorale No. 19 (4-part chord sequence), Afro-Cuban clave-style rhythm patterns (bossa-nova, gahu, rumba, shiko, son, soukous — Table 1), and 2D/3D time-delay embeddings of several of the above. The consistent empirical result: **β₀=1, β₁=1 recovers a persistent circle** (`S¹`) in every one of these examples over some ε-interval — i.e. the data-driven barcode independently rediscovers the classical-topology answer from part 1 (circularity of pitch classes, circle of fifths, rhythmic cyclicity). Higher-dimensional Betti numbers (β₂, β₃) appear for time-delay embeddings but the authors explicitly say their musical meaning is unclear ("the meaning of the higher-dimensional Betti numbers is less clear... we suspect once persistent homology of Gaussian fields is better understood it will help").

No deep-learning, no audio signal processing, no numerical eval beyond visual barcode inspection — this is a demonstration/validation paper ("does persistent homology recover topology we already know is there from theory?"), not a benchmark paper. It reports no accuracy numbers, no comparison against any baseline method, no computational cost figures. The circle-of-fifths section (§4.7) is the closest thing to a "surprising confirmation": inputting 12 major-scale note-sets with no circular ordering given as input, the β₁=1 persistent bar appears anyway, matching the standard music-theory circle of fifths — a nice sanity-check result but expected given the ordering is baked into the chord-class distance metric's structure.

**What it does NOT contain:**
- **No audio at all** — everything operates on MIDI note-level (pitch, onset time) data; nothing about spectrograms, latents, waveforms, or any signal-derived feature space. Zero relevance to SAME's continuous latent geometry or MERT/Audiobox-style embeddings.
- **No learned/trained model of any kind.** No neural network, no probe, no classifier — this is descriptive/structural math applied to hand-extracted symbolic events.
- **No control or steering mechanism.** The paper analyzes existing musical data's shape; it does not propose using topology to *generate*, *condition*, or *guide* anything. There is nothing here that maps onto LatCH-style control heads or FiLM/cross-attn conditioning.
- **No generative-modeling evaluation metric.** The persistent-homology barcodes are used as a descriptive/exploratory visualization, not validated as a discriminative or quality metric (no FAD-like correlation study, no human-listening correlation).
- **No claim about learned embedding spaces.** All topological claims are about hand-specified metrics on hand-specified (pitch, time) coordinates — nothing here says anything about whether a trained audio embedding space (chroma, HPCP, MERT, SAME latent) actually exhibits circular/toroidal structure; that would need to be independently verified on our own feature spaces if this line of inquiry were pursued.
- **Dated & narrow-genre input set.** All symbolic examples are Western tonal (scales, hymnal chorale, folk tune) plus one Turkish makam and six Afro-Cuban clave patterns — no polyphonic pop/electronic/dance material, nothing resembling the Goa-trance corpus this project trains on.

**Bottom line for SAO:** this is a solid, honest, self-contained math-methods paper (13 references, careful proofs, explicit caveats about the limits of its own higher-Betti-number interpretability) but it is a pure symbolic-MIDI theory piece from 2013 with no audio, no ML, and no control mechanism. Its only conceivable relevance to this project is as a citable theoretical grounding for *why* chroma/pitch-class features are naturally circular (already assumed implicitly in our HPCP/chroma-loss code) — it does not suggest any new technique, metric, or architecture change. Filed as a background/off-lane reference, not an actionable lead.
