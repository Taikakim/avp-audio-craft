Confirmed from the PDF header/title page: this IS Dmitri Tymoczko's "Why topology?", *Journal of Mathematics and Music* Vol. 14 No. 2, pp. 114–169, published online 24 Sep 2020, open-access (CC BY-NC-ND). Single author, Princeton Music Department. The flag was correct.

**The actual argument (not persistent homology / TDA — a different sense of "topology"):**
- The paper's "topology" is **algebraic/geometric topology of orbifolds** (homotopy theory, fundamental groups, quotient/orbifold spaces) applied to *voice-leading spaces* — the configuration spaces of *n*-note chords that Tymoczko, Callender and Quinn built in their 2008 *Science* paper ("Generalized Voice-Leading Spaces") and Tymoczko's 2011 book *A Geometry of Music*. It has **nothing to do with persistent homology, simplicial complexes built from data, or topological data analysis** — it is pure music theory using topology as a descriptive/organizing language, not a data-analysis tool.
- Core technical move: chords are points in an *n*-dimensional orbifold **𝕋ⁿ/𝐒ₙ** (an *n*-torus quotiented by voice-permutation symmetry) — "a twisted, mirrored, higher-dimensional orbifold." Voice leadings between chords are paths through this space; because the space has nontrivial topology (circular dimensions + mirror boundaries), paths fall into **homotopy classes**, and the paper's central claim is that these homotopy classes correspond exactly to familiar music-theoretic transformations: "transpositions along the chord," "voice exchanges," and "generalized neo-Riemannian transformations" (inversions).
- The paper builds **simplified two-dimensional models** of these higher-dimensional spaces for practical use: (1) **annular (donut) diagrams** — deemphasize voice-crossings, keep the circular/transpositional dimension, used to show that classic diagrams like the *Tonnetz*, Vial's 18th-century key-relations chart, and Lerdahl's diatonic-triad torus all secretly encode the same annular topology, explaining the long-unexplained difference between "trivial" and "nontrivial" loops on the *Tonnetz* (§1–2); (2) **polygonal set-class-space diagrams** (an *n*-sided polygon per *n*-note chord type) — deemphasize the circular/transpositional dimension, used to formalize both *transpositional* set-class space (§3) and *inversional* set-class space (§4), generalizing Cohn's neo-Riemannian L/P/R triad transformations to chords of any cardinality and any interval content (not just maximally-even/near-diatonic chords, which prior geometric-music-theory work had been restricted to — this generality claim is the paper's stated main contribution, per the abstract: "enrich set theory's contrapuntal power by simplifying the description of its geometry").
- §5 distills all of this into a small algebraic "alphabet" — *n* voice-exchanges, *n* transpositions-along-the-chord, *n* neo-Riemannian inversions per *n*-note chord — that lets a path through the topological space be read off as a short word (e.g. `t₂c₀i₂`), giving a structure-neutral notation applicable to any chord in any scale.
- Analytical payoff sections work through real repertoire with this apparatus: the *Tristan* prelude's opening half-diminished-to-dominant-seventh voice leading (analyzed as a sequence of boundary interactions / transformational "moves," §3, reprised in §5's alphabet); passages from Mozart K.309, Schnittke's *Requiem* Benedictus, Gesualdo's *Moro Lasso* (parallel-voice inversional patterns, §4); Schoenberg's *Book of the Hanging Gardens* and a descending-fifth dominant sequence (both modeled as the same path in set-class space, §3); Stravinsky's *Rite of Spring* (016-trichord and inversionally-related tetrachord passages, via Jon Russell's dissertation, §4); Wagner's *Ring*/*Parsifal*, Chopin, Tchaikovsky are cited via a companion configuration-theory paper, not analyzed here directly.
- §6 (Conclusion) makes a philosophical distinction the paper treats as its deeper payoff: **global symmetries** (operations you can do *to* a passage of music, e.g. transposing/inverting an entire piece) versus **contrapuntal possibilities / the fundamental group** (moves you can make *inside* a piece, i.e. voice leadings) — attributed to David Lewin's transformational-theory framing ("something you can do inside a piece" vs. "to a piece"). Tymoczko argues topology lets you keep both a realist geometric methodology *and* transformational theory's simplifying power, rather than treating them as rival philosophies.
- Explicit stated caveats/limitations from the author himself: inversional set-class space (§4) is argued to be useful mainly for passages that are *literally or nearly inversionally related* — the author is "much less confident about its ability to relate transpositions" and notes the mostly-atonal composers who favor inversional categorization are the ones *least* concerned with efficient voice leading, and vice versa (p.150, explicit self-limitation). Several technical constructs (behavior of voice leadings passing through the singular "tip" of the orbifold cone, the topological non-uniqueness of the graphical polygon interactions at cone points) are flagged as unresolved / "a matter for a future paper" (footnotes 47–48). The Mathematical Appendix is present but the author explicitly does not give lemmas/theorems/proofs in the main text, deferring rigor to the appendix or prior literature (Callender/Quinn/Tymoczko 2008; Tymoczko 2011).

**What it does NOT contain — critical, since the team's actual interest is topology-as-data-analysis:**
- **No persistent homology, no simplicial complexes built from data, no Betti numbers, no filtration, no point-cloud/manifold-learning of any kind.** "Topology" here means classical algebraic topology (fundamental groups, homotopy classes, orbifolds, covering-space-style quotient constructions) applied by hand to a *hand-built discrete symbolic space* (12-tone-equal-temperament pitch classes / chords), not a computational method run on real signals or embeddings.
- **No audio, no spectral/timbral features, no MIR pipeline, no waveform or latent-space representation whatsoever.** Everything is discrete pitch-class arithmetic (mod-12, or mod-*o* for a general scale) done symbolically on score excerpts (rendered as musical notation figures). There is no bridge to SAME's 256-d continuous latent space, no chroma/HPCP feature that could be repurposed as a training target, no distance metric usable as a loss.
- **No algorithm, embedding, or evaluation methodology transferable to controllable audio generation.** The "alphabet" (t/c/i letters) is a notation for describing existing voice-leading moves post hoc in a musical score — not a generative or steerable representation, and not differentiable in any sense.
- **No machine learning of any kind** — the paper predates and is unrelated to any neural/embedding-based methods; it is closer in spirit to abstract algebra / classical topology applied to 12-tone music theory (in the same lineage as Cohn's neo-Riemannian theory, the *Tonnetz*, and Lewin's *Generalized Musical Intervals and Transformations*) than to anything resembling topological data analysis (TDA), persistence diagrams, or feature-extraction pipelines.
