# **Architectural Magnitude-Control and Normalization in Pretrained Flow-Matching Diffusion Transformers**

## **The Pathology of Scale Runaway in Pre-LayerNorm Flow-Matching Architectures**

The transition from convolutional U-Nets to Diffusion Transformers (DiTs) has fundamentally altered the training dynamics of generative models. When coupled with rectified flow-matching objectives and scaling to the 1.4-billion parameter regime (e.g., the SA3 architecture), deep Pre-LayerNorm (Pre-LN) networks exhibit a pronounced vulnerability to output-scale runaway during full fine-tuning. This pathology manifests as a catastrophic divergence in the per-channel standard deviation of predicted latents, often escalating from a stable baseline of 1.0 to 5.6, and rapidly degrading into non-finite values and pure noise. While extensive hyperparameter tuning involving weight decay and gradient clipping is the conventional recourse, an emerging body of literature proposes purely architectural magnitude-control mechanisms designed to bound activations and weights by construction.  
This report provides an exhaustive evaluation of these architectural interventions, focusing strictly on their viability for mid-training retrofitting onto a pretrained, non-normalized DiT. The analysis isolates mechanisms from EDM2, nGPT, NFNets, ![][image1]Reparam, and modern QK-normalization strategies. The core objective is to identify which techniques can safely stabilize a pretrained model without precipitating representation collapse or requiring a from-scratch retraining phase, and to definitively answer whether these architectural changes render weight decay and gradient clipping obsolete in the context of fine-tuning.  
Before evaluating the proposed solutions, it is imperative to dissect the mechanical origins of activation runaway in the target architecture. The specified model is a 1.4B parameter DiT utilizing standard Pre-LN, AdaLN-Zero conditioning, Rotary Positional Embeddings (RoPE), and Flash Attention, trained via a rectified-flow objective targeting a unit-variance latent space. The failure mode is not a simple learning rate overshoot, but rather a structural vulnerability exacerbated by the interaction between the network's normalization scheme and the geometry of the flow-matching objective.

### **Residual Stream Variance Accumulation**

In standard Pre-LN architectures, the normalization operation is applied exclusively to the input of the sub-layer (whether attention or the feed-forward network), while the main residual stream remains completely unbounded1. Consequently, the variance of the hidden states grows monotonically with depth. If the input to the network has a specific variance, the variance at layer ![][image2] approximates the sum of the initial variance and the accumulated variance of all preceding sub-layer outputs3. While this linear or exponential variance accumulation is generally manageable during pretraining due to careful learning rate warmup schedules and variance-preserving weight initializations, fine-tuning introduces localized, high-magnitude gradient updates.  
These updates disproportionately inflate the weight norms of specific layers, which then cascades through the unbounded residual stream. This phenomenon results in "massive activations" that act as dominant biases, steering the network's representation into a narrow subspace and eventually exceeding the representable range of half-precision formats like FP16 or BF162. When the residual stream overflows, the subsequent normalization layers fail, propagating non-finite values through the network.

### **The Role of AdaLN-Zero Conditioning**

The AdaLN-Zero mechanism, a hallmark of DiT architectures, initializes the final linear projection of the conditioning multi-layer perceptron (MLP) to zero. This ensures that the shift, scale, and residual gate parameters begin as identity functions, providing a stable, residual-like initialization7. However, during fine-tuning, the network often learns to aggressively modulate the scale parameters to fit new conditional distributions.  
Without structural bounds on these regression weights, the scale parameters can drift exponentially. Because these scale parameters multiplicatively modulate the normalized tokens before they enter the attention and feed-forward blocks, an inflated scale parameter instantly injects extreme magnitudes into the sub-layers. The sub-layers then process these magnified inputs, apply their own unconstrained weight projections, and add the result back into the main residual stream, creating a feedback loop of compounding magnitude growth.

### **Flow-Matching and Velocity Target Geometry**

The target model utilizes a rectified flow-matching objective, predicting the velocity vector ![][image3], where ![][image4] represents the data and ![][image5] represents the standard Gaussian noise7. Recent geometric analyses of flow matching reveal that velocity prediction fundamentally differs from clean data prediction in its error landscape12. Specifically, predicting the velocity vector imposes an isotropic unit-variance floor across all latent directions.  
If the underlying data manifold is anisotropic—meaning it possesses low-variance directions inherent to the encoded visual latents—the velocity prediction objective forces the network to predict these directions with artificially inflated magnitudes to match the isotropic noise component12. During a full fine-tune, the optimizer struggles to reconcile the pretrained weight geometry with new data distributions under this isotropic penalty. The optimizer frequently responds by aggressively scaling up the output projection weights to minimize the velocity matching loss, thereby triggering the observed output standard deviation explosion. The weight norms decouple from their ideal directions, growing uncontrollably to satisfy the variance requirements of the flow-matching target.

## **Exhaustive Sweep of Architectural Magnitude Controls**

The contemporary research landscape offers several architectural magnitude controls. However, a critical distinction must be drawn between methods that are mathematically safe to drop into an ongoing training run and those that fundamentally alter the representation space, necessitating a full retraining phase. A retrofit is only mathematically safe if it satisfies the Condition of Initial Equivalence: the output of the layer immediately following the architectural change must be identical, within floating-point precision, to the original layer.

### **The EDM2 Framework: Forced Weight Normalization and Magnitude Preservation**

Karras et al. (2024) introduced the EDM2 architecture, which systematically eliminates magnitude drift by redesigning all network layers to be magnitude-preserving on expectation13. The framework decomposes into several distinct ingredients, each with vastly different implications for retrofitting.  
The cornerstone of EDM2 is Forced Weight Normalization (FWN). FWN replaces standard linear and convolutional weights with a normalized counterpart, completely decoupling the directional update of the weight matrix from its magnitude. In practice, the weight matrix is divided by its own Frobenius norm and scaled by a factor related to the fan-in dimension15. The EDM2 authors assert that this structural bound eliminates the need for weight decay, learning rate warmup, and scale tuning by explicitly preventing weight norm drift14.  
Evaluating FWN for retrofit safety reveals that it is moderately safe, but only if applied with strict mathematical compensations. If applied natively to a pretrained weight matrix, the forward pass will immediately shift because the pretrained weight norm is forcibly scaled to a theoretical ideal. To retrofit safely, the normalization must be augmented with a learnable scalar, initialized exactly to the original norm of the pretrained weight matrix. This achieves exact mathematical equivalence at step zero, while subjecting all future updates to the normalized directional constraint. However, while FWN bounds the contribution of the specific layer and prevents local weight norm explosion, it does not strictly bound the accumulated variance within the unbounded residual stream.  
EDM2 also proposes replacing standard activations and summations with magnitude-preserving variants, such as MP-SiLU (which subtracts the non-zero mean of SiLU and scales the variance) and MP-sum (which divides residual additions by the square root of two)15. Retrofitting these specific components is fatal to a pretrained model. Dividing a pretrained residual addition by a constant instantly halves the magnitude of the signal, destroying the calibrated entropy of the latent representation. MP-SiLU similarly introduces an immediate activation shift. These are strictly from-scratch mechanisms that cannot be applied mid-training. Furthermore, EDM2 advocates for the removal of learnable scales in normalization layers, which is also fatal to retrofit, as the pretrained model relies heavily on these learned affine parameters to route information. Finally, EDM2 introduces a post-hoc Exponential Moving Average (EMA) technique to optimize the EMA profile after training14. While highly valuable for evaluating model checkpoints, this technique does not influence the forward-pass magnitude dynamics during the fine-tuning process itself.

### **nGPT: Hyperspherical Normalization**

Loshchilov et al. (2024) introduced nGPT, an architecture that places all token embeddings, residual streams, and weights onto the unit hypersphere18. By mathematically enforcing unit length at every step, nGPT eradicates the residual variance explosion commonly known as the "Curse of Depth"20.  
The mechanism continuously projects the residual stream back to the hypersphere, scaling it by a dimension-specific constant. The corresponding optimization strategy, Magnitude-Direction (MD) decoupling, factorizes each weight into a fixed-norm direction and learnable per-row and per-column magnitude gains19. The authors provide compelling evidence that this formulation completely removes the need for weight decay and warmup, as the optimizer operates strictly on angles and independent magnitude scalars24.  
Despite these theoretical advantages, retrofitting nGPT onto a pretrained Pre-LN DiT is mathematically impossible without catastrophic forgetting. A pretrained Pre-LN network relies heavily on the relative magnitude of tokens in the residual stream to encode importance and attention distributions. Projecting a pretrained Euclidean representation onto a unit hypersphere destroys this magnitude-encoded information. While nGPT exhibits exceptional convergence speedups and stability, there is no mathematically sound way to dynamically map a standard pretrained space into a hyperspherical space mid-training26. It remains exclusively a from-scratch architecture.

### **Scaled Weight Standardization in Normalizer-Free Networks**

Brock et al. (2021) introduced Normalizer-Free Networks (NFNets), which eliminate batch and layer normalization entirely by using Scaled Weight Standardization29. This technique reparameterizes weights to have zero mean and unit variance, subsequently multiplied by a non-linearity-specific gain factor designed to preserve signal variance across depths30.  
While effective in from-scratch pretraining, Scaled Weight Standardization is highly unsafe for retrofitting. Standardizing a pretrained weight matrix shifts both its mean and its variance. Unlike simple normalization, which preserves the directional vector of the weights, mean-centering the weights fundamentally alters the linear projection hyperplane33. If a pretrained weight matrix is mean-centered, the output distribution shifts drastically, instantly spiking the loss. Furthermore, the NFNet architecture strictly requires Adaptive Gradient Clipping (AGC) to train stably, refuting any hypothesis that this specific architectural change removes the need for gradient clipping29. Scaled Weight Standardization is designed to work in tandem with variance-preserving initializations and cannot function as a drop-in stabilizer for a pretrained network experiencing runaway.

### **![][image1]Reparam and Spectral Normalization**

Zhai et al. (2023) identified that attention entropy collapse in deep transformers is driven by the unconstrained growth of the spectral norm of attention projection matrices. They proposed ![][image1]Reparam, which reparameterizes linear layers by dividing the weight matrix by its spectral norm, estimated continuously via power iteration, and multiplying by a learnable scalar37.  
This mechanism effectively bounds the Lipschitz constant of the linear layer, preventing any single matrix from acting as an exponential amplifier of the residual stream38. The authors claim this stabilization removes the need for learning rate warmup and adaptive optimizers in some settings, though empirical evidence suggests weight decay remains beneficial for generalization38.  
Crucially, ![][image1]Reparam is entirely safe for retrofitting. The method explicitly supports initializing the learnable scalar to the exact spectral norm of the pretrained weight matrix at step zero. Because it preserves the exact linear transformation at initialization, the model experiences zero distribution shift on the first forward pass. However, while it prevents the matrix from functioning as an exponential amplifier, it does not bound the absolute output magnitude if the input—the residual stream—is already exhibiting extreme drift. It is a highly effective layer-wise constraint, but not a holistic solution to residual runaway.

### **QK-Norm and Tanh Soft-Capping**

To prevent attention logit blowup, recent architectures including Gemma-2, Stable Diffusion 3, and Flux apply normalization (either LayerNorm or RMSNorm) to the Query and Key vectors prior to the dot product40. In some implementations, this is followed by a Tanh soft-capping function that bounds the maximum and minimum values of the logits.  
By bounding the magnitude of the Query and Key vectors, the resulting attention logits remain within a numerically stable range, preventing the softmax operation from collapsing into a hard maximum—a state known as entropy collapse. Retrofitting QK-Norm is safe, provided the normalization is accompanied by learnable affine parameters initialized to match the empirical variance of the pretrained Query and Key vectors. If applied without this compensation, the attention distribution flattens instantly, destroying the learned attention maps and spiking the loss.  
However, QK-Norm strictly stabilizes the attention distribution. It has absolutely zero direct effect on the magnitude of the residual stream or the final feed-forward output41. It successfully neutralizes a specific failure mode (attention collapse) but does nothing to cure unbounded weight norm growth in the output projections or the accumulation of variance in the main stream.

### **Residual-Stream Magnitude Control Strategies**

Efforts to control the residual stream magnitude directly have yielded mixed results for retrofitting. As established, the EDM2 approach of dividing the residual addition by a constant is destructive to pretrained weights. Other techniques, such as LayerScale or ReZero, introduce a learnable scalar initialized to zero (or a very small value) on the residual branch43.  
Retrofitting LayerScale by inserting a scalar initialized to 1.0 is mathematically safe, as it satisfies initial equivalence. However, initializing the scalar to 1.0 completely defeats the purpose of the mechanism, which relies on starting near zero to maintain an identity mapping early in training. Inserting a scalar of 1.0 does not bound growth; it merely provides the network an additional parameter that it *could* theoretically use to suppress growth. Given the isotropic variance penalty of the flow-matching objective, the optimizer is highly unlikely to utilize this scalar to suppress magnitude, as doing so would contradict the gradient signals demanding larger velocity predictions. Recent literature continues to highlight that residual variance growth remains a fundamental architectural limitation of Pre-LN networks, requiring careful pretraining rather than post-hoc residual scaling5.

### **Flow-Matching Specifics in Stable Diffusion 3 and Flux**

Architectures like Stable Diffusion 3 and Flux utilize the Multimodal Diffusion Transformer (MMDiT) structure, which processes text and image tokens through either shared or decoupled attention streams7. These models heavily rely on RMSNorm placement and QK-norm to maintain stability during pretraining40. The final output layer maps the sequence back to the latent dimension, universally relying on AdaLN-Zero to scale the final projections7.  
Documented cases of instability in flow-matching full fine-tunes reveal that these architectures prevent runaway primarily through massive batch sizes and extreme learning rate constraints during pretraining. When exposed to the high-variance gradients of a fine-tuning dataset—especially when the target distribution introduces novel, anisotropic latent directions—the velocity prediction target severely destabilizes the AdaLN-Zero scale parameters and the final output weights11. The architectural design of SD3 and Flux offers no inherent defense against this specific fine-tuning pathology, confirming that the instability is a fundamental consequence of combining Pre-LN variance growth with unconstrained flow-matching targets.

## **PyTorch and ROCm Implementation Constraints**

Implementing these architectural changes on AMD hardware using ROCm introduces specific systems-level constraints, particularly regarding the use of torch.compile and distributed training frameworks like Distributed Data Parallel (DDP) or Fully Sharded Data Parallel (FSDP).  
The standard PyTorch API for weight reparameterization is torch.nn.utils.parametrize49. However, relying on this module for Forced Weight Normalization or ![][image1]Reparam is highly discouraged in this context. The parametrize module utilizes internal tensor caching and PyTorch forward hooks. On AMD ROCm, these dynamic hooks frequently cause graph breaks in the Triton compiler (torch.compile), forcing execution back to Python eager mode and devastating training throughput51. Furthermore, open issues in the PyTorch repository document gradient tracking failures and severe distributed autograd overhead when parametrize.cached() is used across multi-GPU setups52.  
To ensure optimal performance and avoid graph breaks, the retrofit must be implemented functionally within the forward pass of a custom nn.Module subclass. For Forced Weight Normalization, the implementation must manually compute the vector norm using torch.linalg.vector\_norm, apply the scalar math, and pass the resulting normalized weight directly into the F.linear functional call. This functional approach avoids all hook overhead and is seamlessly compiled by torch.compile into highly efficient Triton kernels on ROCm hardware.

## **The Minimal Retrofit Recipe for Pretrained DiTs**

To rescue the 1.4B Pre-LN DiT experiencing output runaway during rectified-flow fine-tuning, the following architectural retrofit provides the most mathematically sound and computationally efficient path. It relies heavily on dynamically scaled Forced Weight Normalization applied strategically to the most vulnerable layers, bypassing the destructive nature of full architectural rewrites.

### **1\. Target Layers: The Output Projections**

The runaway manifests most aggressively in the predicted latents. In a DiT, the final block maps the residual stream directly to the latent dimension. The primary culprit for scale explosion is the unconstrained weight matrix of this final projection layer, which attempts to satisfy the isotropic variance penalty of the velocity-prediction objective by continuously inflating its weights12. Consequently, the retrofit must target this final nn.Linear projection, and optionally, the MLP ![][image6] output down-projections in the deepest 25% of the transformer blocks, where residual variance is highest.

### **2\. Implementation: Scaled Forced Weight Normalization (SFWN)**

The targeted linear layers must be replaced with a custom module implementing functionally normalized weights.  
During the initialization phase—executed exactly once upon loading the pretrained checkpoint—the algorithm calculates the Frobenius norm of the pretrained weight matrix. A new learnable scalar parameter, ![][image7], is instantiated and initialized to this exact Frobenius norm. The underlying weight matrix remains a standard learnable parameter.  
During the forward pass, implemented carefully to avoid torch.compile graph breaks on ROCm, the module computes the current dimension-scaled norm of the weight matrix. It normalizes the weight by dividing by this norm and multiplies it by the learnable ![][image7] parameter. The resulting effective weight matrix is then passed into the standard linear functional call.

### **3\. Critical Exclusions**

It is absolutely vital to exclude the AdaLN-Zero shift and scale regressors from this normalization scheme. These layers must retain the capability of outputting exact zeros to maintain the identity function at various points in the training trajectory. Forcing their weights to have a normalized magnitude prevents them from reliably outputting near-zero magnitudes, which will severely destabilize the conditioning mechanism. Furthermore, embedding layers must never be normalized, as modifying their structure instantly destroys the calibrated positional and token representations.

### **4\. Training Dynamics Post-Retrofit**

Because this specific retrofit strictly satisfies the Condition of Initial Equivalence, the loss on the first fine-tuning step will be identical to the unmodified model. Consequently, no short learning rate re-warmup is necessary. The optimizer will seamlessly continue training, but it will now update the magnitude (![][image7]) and the directional components of the weight matrix separately. This bounds the magnitude of the projection mapping to the specific velocity space, forcing the network to optimize the direction of the transformation rather than artificially scaling the output to minimize the flow-matching loss.

## **The Final Verdict: The Necessity of Weight Decay and Gradient Clipping**

The central inquiry motivating this analysis is whether an architectural retrofit allows for the complete removal of weight decay and gradient clipping during a full fine-tune. The analysis dictates that the verdict is definitively no.  
While foundational papers such as EDM2 and MD Decoupling14 demonstrate that weight decay and gradient clipping can theoretically be eliminated, these claims apply strictly to from-scratch pretraining regimens where every single layer, residual connection, and activation function is mathematically constrained by construction.  
In a fine-tuning scenario on a pretrained Pre-LN DiT, the network operates within a pre-existing geometric space where the residual stream variance has already grown significantly with network depth3. Retrofitting Forced Weight Normalization to the projection layers treats the symptom—output inflation—by capping the layer's amplification factor, but it does not cure the underlying variance accumulation intrinsic to Pre-LN architectures. Furthermore, rectified flow-matching objectives, particularly those utilizing velocity prediction, inject high-variance stochastic gradients when operating far from the data manifold11. The architectural retrofit cannot prevent the objective function from generating these extreme gradient signals.  
Therefore, for a pragmatic and stable fine-tune of a 1.4B DiT, gradient clipping remains an indispensable safety mechanism to protect the network from batch-specific flow-matching noise. Weight decay should similarly be maintained, though its coefficient can potentially be reduced by an order of magnitude, as the Scaled Forced Weight Normalization retrofit will assume the primary responsibility for preventing weight norm runaway in the output layers. The combination of targeted structural normalization on the output projections alongside moderate gradient clipping and weight decay provides the most robust, computationally efficient, and mathematically sound path to stabilize output-scale runaway without discarding the immense value of the pretrained checkpoint.
