# Mathematical Foundations of Complex Tonality (arXiv:2208.04974, IMA Conf. on Mathematics in Music 2022 workshop) — Jeffrey R. Boland, Lane P. Hughston

**Relevance to SAO: off-lane — pure symbolic tuning-theory / number theory, no audio signal, no geometry-of-timbre, no ML. Not actionable for control-method design, feature extraction, or eval metrics.**
**Status: DEEP-READ (full PDF, 2026-08-12, GHOST-NOTE).**

This is a music-theory/number-theory paper, not a signal-processing or ML paper — it has nothing to do with audio, spectrograms, latents, or learned representations. It addresses the "problem of F♯" in 5-limit just intonation: under just intonation (interval ratios built from powers of the primes 2, 3, 5), the tritone C→F♯ has no single consistent value — depending on how you build it up from thirds/fifths/whole-tones you get 45/32, 64/45, 25/18, or 36/25, none satisfactory, and other notes (D, B♭) similarly split into multiple "wolf"-interval-avoiding values via the syntonic comma κ=81/80.

The paper's actual mechanism: it factorizes the two composite primes that make just intonation awkward — 2 = (1+i)(1−i) and 5 = (1+2i)(1−2i) — over the Gaussian integers ℤ[i], and proposes representing musical tones as ratios of Gaussian integers ("complex tonality") rather than ordinary rationals. The tritone becomes the clean single value 1+i : 1 (magnitude √2, matching equal temperament's √2, but now an exact algebraic object rather than an irrational limit). They build up "complex semitones" (α = ¾(1+i), β = ⅓(3+i), and the ordinary real diatonic semitone Z=16/15) and "complex whole tones" (γ = ½(1+2i), δ = ⅘(1+i), alongside the ordinary X=9/8, Y=10/9), show these can be aggregated ("atoms"/"molecules," an explicit chemistry analogy) into complete chromatic scales in all twelve major/minor keys, and prove classification theorems (Thm 1: exactly 3 families of 3-real-semitone systems reproduce Ptolemy's diatonic scale via aggregation, 96 scales total; Thm 2: exactly 3 families of complex 3-semitone systems satisfying a conjugate-symmetry condition; Thm 3: uniqueness for a 2-semitone complex "Pythagorean" analogue). Formal apparatus: Gaussian-integer unique factorization (mod units ±1,±i and "5-limit phase factors" ζ=(3+4i)/5 built from the Gaussian norm of 5), and a proof that the resulting interval space forms a Lewin (1987) Generalized Interval System (GIS).

The "empirical" content is entirely internal/formal — there are no audio experiments, no listening tests, no acoustic measurements. The closest thing to validation is a closing qualitative discussion (§X) applying the complex system to real repertoire: they re-derive the Tristan chord and claim its intervals (B/F tritone → ξ=1+i, etc.) are uniformly "low-power-of-primes" (consonant by their metric) in the complex system versus "high-power" (dissonant) in plain just intonation, and speculate this explains why the tritone "loses its dissonance" in that chord's context. They also walk the neo-Riemannian hexatonic cycle (C major→C minor→A♭major→...) through their Complex System I and note structural asymmetries between parallel vs. relative minor. This is interpretive/speculative commentary, explicitly flagged by the authors themselves as such ("we can only speculate," "it may be that...", ending with a philosophical Nietzsche quote about "new ears for new music") — not a testable or measured claim.

**What it does NOT contain:** no topology (no persistent homology, no simplicial complexes, no geometric/metric space of chords beyond classical GIS), no signal processing, no spectral/timbral features, no connection to audio at all — this is entirely about discrete tuning ratios for a 12-tone chromatic scale (frequency ratios as algebraic numbers), not about continuous pitch, timbre, rhythm, or any of the LatCH control dimensions (onset density, energy, chroma, spectral features) the SAO project trains heads on. It offers no algorithm, distance metric, embedding, or evaluation methodology that could be repurposed for controllable audio generation, feature extraction, or eval — it's a closed-form symbolic tuning classification exercise with a literary/interpretive coda. The "Gaussian Interval System" formalism (Remark 4, citing Lewin 1987) is the only structure with any generality, but it generalizes *just-intonation ratio arithmetic*, not audio representations — there is no plausible bridge to SAME's continuous latent space, chroma/HPCP conditioning, or the DiT's control adapters. Do not confuse this with the team's actual geometric-music-theory interest (Tymoczko-style orbifolds / voice-leading spaces, or persistent-homology-on-audio-features work) — this paper shares only surface vocabulary ("tonality," "chords," "Gaussian") with that line of inquiry, not method.
