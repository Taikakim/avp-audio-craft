**Status: DEEP-READ (full PDF, all 37 pages, 2026-08-12, GHOST-NOTE).**

The paper re-derives the classical Euler/neo-Riemannian **tonnetz** (the triangular/hexagonal lattice of the 24 major/minor triads in 12-tone equal temperament) as three mathematically equivalent objects and shows they're literally isomorphic:

1. **A finite bipartite Levi graph** of degree 3, girth 6, order 24 — 12 white vertices (major triads), 12 black vertices (minor triads), 36 edges corresponding to the three neo-Riemannian operations **P** (parallel), **R** (relative), **L** (Leittonwechsel), each of which changes exactly one pitch of the triad by a semitone or tone while fixing the other two.
2. **A geometric point-line configuration** `{12₃}` in ℝ² (12 points, 12 lines, 3 points/line, 3 lines/point) — every combinatorial configuration of this incidence type corresponds one-to-one with a Levi graph (Grünbaum 2009), and the authors identify the tonnetz's specific configuration as **D222 of Daublebsky von Sterneck (1895)**, one of 229 known `{12₃}` configurations, and prove it is self-dual (Proposition 2).
3. The classical **triangular/hexagonal plane tessellations** (Figs. 1–2), which everyone already knows as "the tonnetz."

The actual mathematical content is enumerative/combinatorial graph theory over these finite structures: counting cycles of the Levi graph by length and "p-number" (number of parallel-transformation edges crossed), tabulated exhaustively in an appendix (Table I: the Eulerian tonnetz has exactly **5409 total cycles**, of which 16 are hexacycles — four "3p-hexacycles" = the classical hexatonic systems, twelve "2p-hexacycles" = Cohn's LPR "pitch-retention loops"). They extend the construction to (a) two "**Archimedean tonnetze**" (bipartite graphs built from two-tone-altering triad transitions, which tile the plane as {4,6,12} dodecagon/hexagon/square tessellations, Table II: 107 total cycles) and (b) a "**Tristan-genus tonnetz**" over the 12 dominant-seventh and 12 minor-sixth tetrachords, constrained by requiring each transition to preserve the tetrachord's "dissonant tone" — this turns out to realize a *different* `{12₃}` configuration, **D228** (Proposition 3, self-dual, Table III: 5125 total cycles).

The bulk of the paper (Sections IV, VI — roughly half the pages) is close score-reading analysis using these graphs as a "road map": tracing the Tristan chord's resolution paths in Wagner's *Tristan und Isolde* (opening bars, the Liebesnacht duet), Chopin's Ballade in G minor Op. 23, Tchaikovsky's Symphony No. 6 "Pathétique" (4th mvt), Wagner's *Parsifal* (Act 3) and *Götterdämmerung* (funeral-pyre scene), reading each chord progression as a walk along a hexacycle/octacycle/decacycle/Hamiltonian-cycle of the relevant Levi graph and using graph-distance ("polar"/"subpolar" position on a cycle) as a proxy for the harmonic "finality" or "tension" of a given resolution. E.g. they show Tymoczko's list of 8 canonical Tristan-chord resolutions corresponds exactly to two of the three 4p-octacycles of the D228 configuration, and that Chopin's resolution (to B♭7) is a full traversal to the "polar" chord on a 2p-decacycle while Wagner's first resolution (to E7) is a single adjacent step on a 3p-hexacycle — offered as an explanation for why Chopin's cadence reads as "definitive" and Wagner's as "unsettled."

**What it does NOT contain:** No persistent homology, simplicial complexes, or any technique from algebraic/computational topology — despite "topology" appearing once in passing ("aspects of the topology of voice-leading spaces," referencing Tymoczko's separate work, not developed here) and despite the MSC tags including 14N20 (which is classical/enumerative algebraic geometry — incidence configurations — not topology). No audio signal, no spectral/timbral features, no neural representations, no latent spaces, no learned embeddings of any kind. No continuous parameters at all — everything is a finite, discrete, symbolic structure over 12-tone equal-temperament pitch classes and triad/tetrachord labels; there is no mechanism here that generalizes to continuous audio-feature manifolds (chroma vectors, MERT embeddings, onset envelopes) as used in SA3's LatCH/control-adapter work. No generative or predictive model, no evaluation metric proposal, no algorithm that produces or scores audio. The "configuration" formalism is 19th-century combinatorial/projective geometry (points-and-lines incidence structures, self-duality, classification tables from Daublebsky von Sterneck 1894/1895 and later completions by Gropp), not a geometric-deep-learning or manifold-learning method — nothing here is differentiable or learnable. In short: this is a music-theory/music-analysis paper for symbolic tonal harmony, not a signal-processing, ML, or topology-of-data paper, and it offers no actionable primitive (loss function, feature, evaluation metric, or control representation) for SA3's control-adapter/LatCH work on continuous audio latents.

**Corrected verdict (Kim direct via WINTERMUTE, 2026-08-12):** the original off-lane call graded this against "does it process audio?" — Kim's real criterion is "does it define a musically meaningful quantity extractable from audio and steerable?" One piece of this paper clears that bar even though the paper itself never leaves the symbolic domain: **graph-distance-from-tonal-center within the tonnetz/tetrachord Levi graph** (their "polar"/"subpolar" position, used to read a chord's resolution as "definitive" vs "unsettled") is functionally a **harmonic-tension / distance-from-home measure**. Under the 3-part test: (1) computable indirectly — build the same graph over chroma-derived chord estimates instead of score labels, position the current chord on it, measure distance to the local tonal center; (2) yes, "more tension" / "further from resolved" is exactly the kind of thing Kim asks for by ear; (3) it's inherently a per-chord (i.e. time-resolved) quantity already — unlike several other papers in this batch, no windowing hack is needed, it's naturally a sequence. Caveat: this is the LEAST validated part of the paper (score-reading narrative on 5 canonical excerpts, no quantitative harmonic-tension benchmark exists to check the "polar=more final" reading against), and it requires a working chord-estimation front end from audio (which HPCP/chroma-based chord recognition can approximate but doesn't currently exist in this pipeline).
