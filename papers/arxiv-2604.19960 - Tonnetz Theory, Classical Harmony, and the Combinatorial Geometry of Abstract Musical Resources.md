# Tonnetz Theory, Classical Harmony, and the Combinatorial Geometry of Abstract Musical Resources (arXiv:2604.19960, unpublished preprint, dated 2026-04-23) — Jeffrey R. Boland, Lane P. Hughston

**Relevance to SAO: off-lane — pure finite-combinatorics/music-theory paper (Levi graphs of point-line configurations), no persistent homology/TDA, no learned models, no audio, no evaluation metrics, no algorithm exportable to control-adapter design or feature extraction.**
**Status: DEEP-READ (full PDF, 26 pages incl. 2 appendices, 2026-08-12, GHOST-NOTE).**

This is a sequel to the authors' own prior paper (Boland & Hughston 2025, arXiv:2505.08752, "Configurations, Tessellations and Tone Networks" — referred to throughout as "B-H (2025)"), which established that the classic Tonnetz (the triangular/hexagonal tessellation used since Euler to depict pitch-class and triad relations) is combinatorially the **Levi graph of the Daublebsky von Sterneck configuration {12₃}, type D222** — a finite, non-planar, 3-regular bipartite incidence structure of 12 major-triad points and 12 minor-triad lines (equivalently 12 pitch-classes and 12 triads), each point on exactly 3 lines and vice versa, with girth ≥6 (no "shortcuts"). This paper extends that same machinery — finite point/line configurations `{n_k}` and their bipartite Levi graphs — to four more musical resource-sets, entirely by hand-constructed combinatorics, with zero code, zero data, zero learned/statistical component:

1. **Diatonic triads** (7 pitches × 7 scale-degree triads of a major/minor key): shown to form a biregular bipartite graph of type `{7₃}` but with **girth four**, i.e. it contains 4-cycles (tetracycles) and is therefore *not* a proper Levi graph/configuration in the strict sense — the paper is explicit about this (Prop. 1). A companion hexagonal tessellation of the plane visualizes it.
2. **Diatonic seventh chords** (7 pitches × 7 seventh-chords of a key, mapped via root+third+seventh rather than root+third+fifth): forms the **Fano configuration {7₃}**, girth six, whose Levi graph is the well-known **Heawood graph** (Prop. 2). Notable because the Fano plane is literally PG(2,2), the projective plane over GF(2).
3. **Pentatonic music**: for a 5-note scale, all `C(5,2)=10` two-note "major" clusters and `C(5,3)=10` three-note "minor" clusters form the **Desargues configuration {10₃}** (Prop. 3), generalized in Prop. 4 to any scale of odd cardinality m=2k+1 via a k-to-(k+1) subset-inclusion map between k-chord and (k+1)-chord sets (`m=7` → `{35₄}`, `m=9` → `{125₅}`, etc. — pure binomial-coefficient combinatorics, no music-specific content beyond "pick a scale size").
4. **12-tone/dodecaphonic music**: fixing an unordered hexachord (6 of the 12 pitch classes) and applying Sylvester's 19th-century "duads and synthemes" combinatorics (15 unordered pairs from the hexachord, 15 ways to partition those 15 pairs into non-overlapping triples) yields the **Cremona-Richmond configuration {15₃}**, whose Levi graph is **Tutte's 8-cage** (Prop. 5).
5. Section V shows the original Eulerian tonnetz (D222) can *also* be derived from plain set-inclusion (pitch ∈ triad) rather than voice-leading/parsimony relations (Props. 6–7) — i.e., the same D222 structure falls out whether you start from "smooth voice leading between chords" or just "which notes are in which chord," which the authors present as a small unifying observation.

Every construction is illustrated with hand-drawn circulant/Levi-graph figures, a plane tessellation, and — as a flourish — two short original toy compositions ("On the Perimeter" for piano, based on a Hamiltonian cycle of the pentatonic Desargues graph; "Decacycle for Violin," based on a 5p-decacycle of the Cremona-Richmond graph) offered as proof-of-concept that these graphs can literally be read off as chord-progression scores. Appendix B tabulates exhaustive cycle counts (by length and crossing number) for the Fano/Heawood, Desargues, and Cremona-Richmond Levi graphs — pure graph enumeration, presumably done by a discrete search over the (small, ≤30-vertex) graphs, not disclosed as code.

**What it does NOT contain** (and the SAO team should not assume otherwise, since the reading sweep's premise is "topology/geometry-of-music methods"):
- **No persistent homology, no simplicial complexes, no filtration, no topological data analysis of any kind.** This is finite/discrete combinatorial geometry (point-line incidence configurations and their Levi graphs), a completely different mathematical toolkit from TDA. If the reading sweep's hypothesis is "could persistent-homology-style methods inform control/feature-extraction," this paper contributes nothing directly — it's geometry-of-music in the classical-configuration-theory sense (Coxeter, Grünbaum), not topology-of-music in the Betti-number/Mapper sense.
- **No continuous/audio representation whatsoever.** Everything is symbolic: discrete pitch classes, triad labels, chord names. There is no spectral, latent, or signal-level construction — nothing that maps onto SAME's continuous latent space, DiT conditioning tokens, or any embedding a control head could regress against.
- **No learned model, no dataset, no training, no evaluation metric, no numeric performance claim of any kind** (the only "numbers" are exact combinatorial counts — e.g. 15 duads/synthemes, 144 Hamiltonian cycles, 90 octacycles, the Appendix B cycle tables — deterministic properties of small finite graphs, not empirical results).
- **No algorithm that generalizes past small, exactly-structured symbolic sets.** The constructions are hand-verified for specific finite cardinalities (5, 7, 12 pitch classes; hexachords of size 6); there's no indication of, or interest in, scaling this to continuous or high-dimensional feature spaces the way a control-adapter or LatCH head would need.
- **No discussion of generative modeling, control, steering, or evaluation** — the intended use case stated by the authors is purely analytical/compositional (a "resource" for human composers and music theorists to draw chord progressions from combinatorial cycles), not a computational method for a generative system.

Net assessment: this is a well-executed but narrow piece of recreational/pedagogical mathematical-music-theory (configuration theory applied to classical/pentatonic/dodecaphonic harmony), continuing the authors' own B-H (2025) line of work. It could be a *very* indirect conceptual nudge — e.g. "chord/pitch relations admit compact discrete graph encodings with provable regularity properties" — for someone designing a symbolic chroma/harmony feature space, but it offers no method, metric, or empirical result that transfers to controllable audio generation, latent-space diagnostics, or persistent-homology-flavored evaluation. Recommend filing as background/tangential, not actionable.
