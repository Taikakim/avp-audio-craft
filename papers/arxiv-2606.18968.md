# Audio-to-Audio via Diffusion Warm Initialization (2606.18968v1) — light pass

Cristóbal Andrade and Sebastian J. Schlecht, DAFx26 2026.
PDF: `2606.18968v1-Audio-to-Audio via Diffusion Warm Initialization.pdf`.
A systematic empirical study of SDEdit-style warm-start diffusion (timbre transfer, MIDI-to-real, enhancement) on Stable Audio Open — mechanism already shipped in SAO's Longform, but the tuning methodology may be worth borrowing.

## What it contains
- Frames "warm initialization" generically: instead of starting reverse diffusion from pure noise x_T, start from a guide signal x^(g) at intermediate timestep t_init = (1-tau_init)*T, optionally adding Gaussian noise scaled by lambda in [0,1]. This is exactly SDEdit (Meng et al. 2022, their own ref [5]).
- Backbone is Stable Audio Open. The paper's background section (2.1) generically describes DDPM/score-based diffusion with two possible noise schedules (VE and VP) and two possible parameterizations (epsilon-prediction or score-function), but explicitly states the specific choice made for this work is the VE (variance-exploding) schedule; the paper does not explicitly confirm which parameterization Stable Audio Open itself uses. CFG guidance is fixed at omega=30 during transfer (vs. the omega=7 training/generation default) — not swept across a range. Not rectified flow, and not the SAME autoencoder latent.
- Introduces two metrics to map the realism-faithfulness tradeoff as a function of tau_init: pitch-based Jaccard Distance (JD, melodic faithfulness to the guide) and Fréchet Audio Distance (FAD, realism/alignment to target distribution, using LAION-CLAP embeddings as in Stable Audio's own eval).
- Empirical sweep across oboe-to-piano and string-to-clarinet timbre transfer finds a joint sweet spot around tau_init≈0.8 (JD<0.6 and FAD<0.7), calling this a practical rule of thumb.
- Key empirical claim: lambda=0 (no explicit noise added to the guide before starting reverse diffusion — guide treated directly as if it were already a sample from the intermediate marginal p_t) performs as well as or better than lambda=1 (full noise re-injection at that timestep); added noise tends to increase "generative hallucinations" without improving distributional alignment.
- Demonstrates the same framework, unmodified, on MIDI-to-real synthesis and audio enhancement (declipping, denoising, interference suppression) — purely qualitative spectrogram comparisons, no metrics for these.
- Discussion section is candid about limitations: fails on out-of-domain guides (humming, sketch-to-sound), fails at source separation/suppression tasks (unwanted components get distorted rather than removed), hyperparameters (tau_init, lambda, omega) are diffusion-model-specific and require re-tuning per backbone, and there is no theoretical account of why tau_init should sit where it does — purely empirical/metric-driven guidance.

## What this means for us
- The core mechanism (partial-noise-then-reverse-diffuse from a guide) is not new to SAO: it is precisely what Longform's SDEdit re-anchor already does (`stable_audio_3/inference/longform.py`), and this paper's own citations acknowledge SDEdit as prior art. No new capability here.
- Both hard filters fail as-shipped: the paper's diffusion equations are DDPM/VE, not rectified-flow velocity fields, and the backbone's latent is Stable Audio Open's own VAE, not SAME's 256-dim/10.77Hz linear-decodable bottleneck. Any of their specific numeric findings (e.g., omega=30 needed vs. omega=7 training default) are backbone-specific and not directly portable.
- The one thing that could be worth stealing is methodological, not mechanistic: their JD (melodic faithfulness) + FAD (realism) grid-search-over-skip-fraction recipe for empirically choosing an SDEdit re-anchor timestep, rather than hand-tuning by ear. This would need re-expression in SA3's own terms — e.g. sweeping tau_init against SA3's rectified-flow t axis (already truncated at 0.075 for crispness), scoring faithfulness via SAO's existing LatCH chroma/onset probes instead of MELODIA pitch-JD, and realism via whatever FAD-analog SAO already tracks. That would formalize (not replace) an already-shipped feature.
- Their negative result — that adding noise back into the guide is often unnecessary and can actively hurt by inducing hallucinated content — is a mild confirmation of standard SDEdit lore, already implicit in how SDEdit re-anchor is typically used; not a new finding for anyone who has already implemented and shipped this mechanism.
- Net: this remains uniquely SAO's already-shipped Longform capability. The paper offers a possible tuning-harness idea, not a new control mechanism or a correction to anything SAO currently believes. Recommend light-abstract filing only; no action item beyond optionally noting the JD+FAD-style tuning pattern in Longform's docs if/when someone revisits re-anchor timestep selection.
