# Spectral Convolution on Orbifolds for Geometric Deep Learning (2602.14997)

_A GDL-theory paper that defines spectral convolution on orbifolds and illustrates it by smoothing a consonance curve on Tymoczko's dyad orbifold — the orbifold hook is real (they cite Tymoczko), but the transferable object for us is a cheap consonance/roughness feature, not the spectral machinery, which has nothing to attach to on our latent. (Subagent deep-read for THE-FINN, 2026-08-12.)_

## What it contains

**Genre.** A short theory paper (Mangliers, Mössner, Himpel; Reutlingen U., Mar 2026). It extends one building block of geometric deep learning — spectral convolution — from manifolds to **orbifolds**, and demonstrates it on a single worked musical example. No network is trained; there are no benchmarks and no numeric results. The "result" is a definition (Theorem 3) plus one illustrative figure sequence.

**What an orbifold is here.** A global quotient `X = M/G`, where `M` is a compact smooth Riemannian manifold and `G` is a compact Lie group acting smoothly, effectively and **almost freely** (all isotropy/stabiliser groups finite). Locally it looks like `ℝⁿ` quotiented by a finite group — a manifold with allowed "cone/mirror" singular points. The G-invariant metric on `M` descends to a Riemannian metric on `X`, so a Laplacian `Δ_X` is well defined, obtained by restricting `Δ_M` to G-invariant functions (`Δ_X f := Δ_M(π*f)`). Its spectrum sits inside that of `Δ_M`.

**The actual math.** On a compact Riemannian manifold, spectral convolution is `(f*g)(x) = Σ_k f̂_k ĝ_k φ_k(x)` where `φ_k` are Laplace–Beltrami eigenfunctions and `f̂_k = ⟨f, φ_k⟩` are generalised Fourier coefficients (multiply in the frequency domain, transform back). **Theorem 3** carries this to orbifolds: because `Δ_X` on a compact orbifold has a discrete spectrum `0 = λ₀ < λ₁ ≤ …→∞` with an orthonormal eigenbasis `{ψ_k}` of `L²(X)`, the Fourier transform `F: L²(X) → ℓ²(ℕ)`, `f ↦ (⟨f,ψ_k⟩)`, is unitary, and convolution is defined by `f*g := F⁻¹(F(f) ⊙ F(g)) = Σ_k f̂(k) ĝ(k) ψ_k`. Well-definedness is a one-paragraph Hilbert-space/Cauchy–Schwarz argument. That is the entire contribution — a clean, coordinate-free convolution on quotient domains, no ad-hoc handling of singular points.

**The worked example — and this is where it touches us.** Section 4 works on the **orbifold of dyads (two-note chords)** `𝒞²₁₂ = 𝕋²₁₂/S₂`. This is exactly the chord-space construction from voice-leading geometry: chord space = `ℝⁿ/Sₙ` (unordered notes → symmetric-group quotient), octave equivalence makes it `𝒞ⁿ_γ = 𝕋ⁿ_γ/Sₙ`, and for n=2 the space is homeomorphic to a **Möbius strip**. The paper states outright that `ℝⁿ/Sₙ` and `𝒞ⁿ_γ` are orbifolds, **citing Tymoczko 2011 and Himpel 2022** (Himpel is a co-author). Eigenfunctions are built from torus eigenfunctions `ψ_{c₁,c₂}(x,y)=exp(2πi/12·(c₁x+c₂y))`, then symmetrised via `Rf=½(f(x,y)+f(y,x))` so only inversion-invariant modes descend to the quotient.

**What they smooth.** A **logarithmic periodicity (consonance) function** `P(p₁,p₂)=log₂(b)`, where `b` is the denominator of the reduced rational frequency ratio — Stolzenburg's periodicity measure of consonance (small = consonant, large = dissonant/complex). They robustify it with a 20-cent JND tolerance (piecewise-constant `P_JND`) and make it inversion-invariant (`P⁺_JND(d)=min{P_JND(d), P_JND(1200−d)}`), yielding a function on `𝒞²₁₂`. They then **spectrally convolve it with a low-pass filter** `g_n` whose Fourier coefficients are `1` for `k≤n` and `0` above (n=529) — i.e. orthogonal projection onto the first `n` orbifold-Laplacian eigenfunctions. Interpretation via **Dirichlet energy**: keeping the lowest eigenvalues = keeping the lowest-energy (smoothest) modes. The output (Fig. 5) is a smoothed consonance-vs-interval curve, essentially a sensory-dissonance/roughness curve with sharp JND steps rounded off. Their argument for doing it on the 2-D orbifold rather than the 1-D interval axis: the Möbius topology encodes inversional symmetry that a 1-D smoothing would ignore.

**Outlook (not done).** Promote the fixed filter to a **trainable** spectral filter (learnable functions of the Laplace spectrum), stack layers with nonlinearities + pooling + readout → a genuine orbifold-CNN. Also flags string-theory (heterotic orbifold) applications. All aspirational; none implemented.

## FOR US

**Three-part relevance test (applied to the one quantity the paper actually produces — the dyad consonance/periodicity function):**
1. **Computable from a rendered clip?** Indirectly. `P⁺_JND` is a static map `dyad → consonance` (a lookup table over intervals). To get a clip quantity you'd run mir's pitch/multipitch extractor, form intervals, evaluate the table, and aggregate. The measure itself is Stolzenburg-2015 periodicity / Sethares roughness — it **predates this paper**; the paper contributes only a smoothing of it.
2. **Would a user ask for it by ear?** Plausibly yes — "more consonant / less dissonant / less rough" is a real audible axis.
3. **Time series or scalar?** Can be a per-frame **roughness/consonance envelope** — a legitimate candidate time-series conditioner. But note: what the paper hands you is a *static field on chord space*, not a timeseries; the timeseries is entirely our construction (evaluate the field along detected pitches over time).

**The Tymoczko-orbifold hook, adjudicated honestly.** It is **not** a coincidence of vocabulary — this is the strong version and the weak version at once. Strong: they work on the *literal same object* as the voice-leading paper (the `ℝⁿ/Sₙ` chord orbifold, the dyad Möbius strip), and cite Tymoczko 2011 for it. Weak: what crosses the bridge is thin for us. Two mismatches: (a) their consonance measure is **Stolzenburg periodicity** (rational-ratio complexity), not Tymoczko's "consonance = distance from the orbifold centre" — same domain, different scalar; the teammate's distance-from-centre reading is not what this paper computes. (b) The paper's *own* contribution — orbifold spectral convolution — is used here only as a **low-pass smoother of a 1-D-ish lookup table**. You do not need Laplacian eigenfunctions on a Möbius strip to get a usable consonance curve; a plain low-pass of Stolzenburg periodicity or a Sethares dissonance integral over the spectrum gives the same shape far more cheaply.

**Does the method port to our RF audio DiT / control heads? No.** Orbifold spectral convolution requires the *data* to live on a known compact manifold-mod-finite-group, with hand-derived Laplacian eigenfunctions (torus/Möbius here). Our learned audio latent is a sequence of VAE vectors with time+channel structure and **no natural finite-group quotient**, so there is nothing for `Δ_X` to attach to and no eigenbasis to build. The machinery is domain-bespoke; it is a tool for building CNNs when your input already is an orbifold (meshes, chord-space histograms), not a general operator you drop on a latent. The trainable-orbifold-CNN is explicitly future work and unimplemented even in the paper.

**Cheapest decisive test, if we want the one real lead (consonance/roughness as a control axis) — and it does NOT use this paper's machinery:** compute a per-frame **Sethares sensory-dissonance / roughness** time series directly from mir's spectrum (no pitch detection, no orbifold), or a Stolzenburg-periodicity envelope over detected pitches; low-pass it; check (i) it tracks a listenable "consonant↔dissonant" axis on a handful of clips and (ii) it's stable enough to be a conditioner/prediction target. If that curve is useful, we adopt the *feature*; the orbifold spectral construction adds nothing a Butterworth filter doesn't. If it isn't useful, the paper offers no rescue.

**Blunt non-transfer notes.** No neural net, no audio, no generative modelling, no numbers — this is a definition paper with one illustrative smoothing. The elegant part (canonical convolution on singular quotients) solves a problem we do not have (data on an orbifold). The part that touches music is a re-derivation of an existing consonance measure on a nice domain. Nothing here is a control-head method for us.

## What stays ours

- **Whether consonance/roughness becomes a control at all**, which measure (Sethares roughness vs Stolzenburg periodicity vs Tymoczko distance-from-centre vs a learned target), and whether it's a whole-track scalar or a per-frame field — all our measurement/decision. The paper picks one (Stolzenburg) for illustration and does not argue it's the right one for conditioning generation.
- **How to make it a timeseries.** The paper gives a static field on chord space; turning it into a per-frame conditioner (pitch tracking → interval stream → aggregation, or spectrum → roughness integral) is entirely our pipeline, and the cheap spectrum-side route sidesteps the orbifold completely.
- **Any smoothing we want** is a filter-design choice we own; orbifold spectral projection is one exotic option with no demonstrated advantage over standard low-pass for our use, at much higher setup cost.
- **The DiT / control-head architecture.** Nothing here changes how we build conditioners or heads; our latent isn't an orbifold, so the paper's building block has no seat.
