# eDiff-I: Text-to-Image Diffusion Models with an Ensemble of Expert Denoisers (2211.01324)

*The canonical "different noise levels do different jobs" paper — its ensemble method is out of budget for our single 1.4B RF fine-tune, but its diagnosis is direct external corroboration of our interval-CFG and timestep-dependent-behavior threads. (Subagent deep-read, THE-FINN 2026-08-12.)*

## What it contains

NVIDIA (Balaji et al., 2022). The paper's premise is a **temporal dynamic** in text-to-image diffusion: the denoiser's job *qualitatively changes* across the sampling trajectory. Early in sampling, when the input is near-pure noise (high σ), the model relies heavily on the text prompt to lay down text-aligned, coarse image structure; late in sampling (low σ), text conditioning is "almost entirely ignored" and the task becomes adding high-frequency visual detail. They validate this two ways:
- **Cross-attention maps (Fig. 3)** across σ = 80, 10.32, 0.72, 0.0005: text (T5/CLIP) attention is strong at high noise, weak at low noise; self-attention over visual features is diffuse at high noise and image-correlated at low noise.
- **Prompt-switching ablation (Fig. 4):** swapping the caption only during the **last 7%** of denoising leaves the output visibly unchanged; swapping during the **first 40%** changes the output completely. Conclusion: the denoiser uses text differently at different noise levels.

**The method (Sec. 4.1 — the headline idea).** Standard practice shares one denoiser across all σ, conditioning on σ only through a time embedding. eDiff-I argues a single limited-capacity net can't capture these distinct regimes, so it trains an **ensemble of "expert denoisers," each specialized to a σ interval**. Crucially this adds *training* parameters but **not per-step inference cost** — at any given σ only one expert runs. To avoid training N experts from scratch, they use a **binary-tree branching** scheme: train one shared model on the full noise distribution p(σ) (log-normal, P_mean = −1.2, P_std = 1.2, EDM/Karras preconditioning), then initialize two children from it and finetune on the low-σ and high-σ halves (p(σ) split equally by area), recursing to level *l* → 2^l intervals. The ideal 2^l-per-level is impractical (model size grows exponentially), and intermediate intervals matter little, so they **grow only the leftmost (low-noise) and rightmost (high-noise) nodes plus one shared model for all middle intervals** — final system = **3 experts** (high-noise expert, low-noise expert, one middle model). High-noise gets a dedicated expert because that is where text-driven image formation happens; low-noise gets one because final sharpness lives there.

**Multiple conditioning (Sec. 4.2):** trains on T5-XXL text + CLIP L/14 text + CLIP L/14 image embeddings jointly (added to time embedding and cross-attended). T5 and CLIP-text favor different image formations; using both gives best results; CLIP-image enables style transfer from a reference. Independent random dropout per embedding; dropping all = unconditional path for classifier-free guidance. Pipeline is a cascade: base 64×64 + SR256 + SR1024.

**paint-with-words (Sec. 4.3):** a *training-free* spatial-control extension — user masks modulate cross-attention via an added attention matrix, with a σ-dependent schedule `w = w'·log(1+σ)·max(QKᵀ)` (larger influence at high noise).

**Results:** zero-shot FID on MS-COCO (Table 1): **eDiff-I-Config-D (9.1B) = 6.95 FID (best in table)**, Config-C (8.1B) = 7.11 — beating Imagen 7.27 (7.9B), Parti 7.23 (20B), DALL·E 2 10.39, Stable Diffusion 8.59 (1.4B), GLIDE 12.24. Ablation (Fig. 7): a **2-expert ensemble trained 600K iters beats the shared baseline trained 800K iters across the entire FID–CLIP trade-off curve** (matched training-sample count), i.e., the gain is from specialization, not more data. Fig. 8: T5+CLIP conditioning dominates T5-only or CLIP-only.

## FOR US

**The method does not transfer — be blunt about it.** eDiff-I buys quality by *adding models*: it trains N experts (from scratch, then branch-finetuned) on ~1B image-text pairs across a cascade on hundreds of A100s. Our situation is the opposite corner: **one ~1.4B rectified-flow audio DiT, fine-tuned on a fixed latent.** We cannot train N experts, cannot branch a tree, and are doing fine-tuning rather than from-scratch pretraining. The ensemble architecture, the cascade, and the branching schedule are all out of budget and out of scope.

**The diagnosis transfers, and it's valuable.** This is arguably *the* canonical, well-instrumented evidence that **conditioning is noise-level-dependent** — the exact premise under our interval-CFG work:
- Their prompt-switch result ("last 7% of denoising: no visible effect") is a clean statement that **text/guidance does essentially nothing near the low-noise end** — so applying CFG there is wasted or actively harmful. External, independent support for our finding that **guidance helps only in a middle noise band** and should not be applied uniformly. *(Cross-ref `arxiv-2404.07724 - Applying Guidance in a Limited Interval…`.)*
- Honest mismatch: eDiff-I's picture is *two-regime* (high-σ = text-driven formation, low-σ = fidelity/detail), monotone in σ. Our interval-CFG picture is *three-regime* (a **middle** band). Not contradictory — eDiff-I doesn't test whether the very-highest-noise steps are also guidance-insensitive, and RF's near-pure-noise regime may behave differently from EDM's. Both land on: **a single CFG scale across all timesteps is the wrong default.**
- For our full-FT "drone" and other **timestep/noise-level-localized behaviors**: eDiff-I supplies both a *mechanism* ("the model does qualitatively different work at different σ, so a behavior can live in a σ band") and a *cheap diagnostic toolkit* — the **prompt-switch ablation** and **cross-attention-vs-σ maps** are inference-time probes, no training required. Fits our lightweight-first ethos: to localize where the drone lives, ablate by noise level rather than retrain.

**Method-lite that we *can* afford (on the one model we have).** The affordable analog of "specialize by noise level" is **not** N experts but **σ-dependent inference-time knobs on a single net** — our interval-CFG already is this. eDiff-I's own paint-with-words uses exactly this trick — a per-σ *schedule* (`w'·log(1+σ)`) on a single shared model, not an ensemble — the template for anything we do here (noise-level-dependent CFG scale / conditioning weight, applied by our one RF model). If we ever wanted "soft specialization" beyond a schedule, a **noise-level-conditioned LoRA/adapter** would be the cheap gesture toward eDiff-I's idea without N full models — speculative, gate behind a cheap probe first.

**RF ≠ EDM caveat.** Their σ schedule (σ_max ≈ 80 → ~0, log-normal weighting) is EDM diffusion; our SA3 is RF with t ∈ [0,1] and a velocity target. The σ→t map is monotone but nonlinear (`t = σ/(1+σ)` — see the EDM note), so **do not port their numeric intervals** (the "7%" / "40%" / σ = 0.72 boundaries). Port the *shape* of the claim (early/high-noise = semantics + conditioning; late/low-noise = detail + conditioning-insensitive) and re-measure the boundaries in our own RF sampler.

## What stays ours

- We remain **a single rectified-flow model.** No ensemble, no cascade, no branching — eDiff-I is corroborating evidence, not a method we adopt.
- **Interval-CFG is ours** and already embodies the transferable insight; eDiff-I raises confidence that the "middle band" is real and physically motivated, but doesn't change what we build.
- The **audio-latent, RF-specific timestep phenomenology** (the drone; where behaviors localize) is ours to characterize empirically. We borrow eDiff-I's *diagnostic* (per-σ prompt-switch / attention ablation) and its *vocabulary* ("noise levels do different jobs"), not its architecture.
- Any noise-level scheduling we do stays **inference-time on one net** (schedules/adapters), never N trained experts.
