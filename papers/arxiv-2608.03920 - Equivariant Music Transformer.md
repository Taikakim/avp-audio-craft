## What it contains
- **Symbolic, not audio.** A 96M-parameter MIDI transformer (12 layers, hidden 768; Lakh MIDI clean subset, ~79k training sequences) that continues a 5 s prompt for 30 s. Each note is a 5-tuple (onset, pitch, duration, instrument, velocity) with a delay pattern.
- **Claim.** Standard music transformers are not equivariant to pitch transposition or onset shift: a shifted input gives uncorrelated representations, and this gets WORSE with model size and training length (Anticipatory baselines: equivariance loss 0.238 -> 0.292 for S 100K -> 800K, 0.397 -> 0.741 for M, 0.495 -> 0.813 for L; lower is better).
- **Fix.** Self-distillation: a second forward pass on a shifted input (pitch -12..+12 semitones, onset shifts up to 5 s), the output distribution inverse-shifted and matched to the unshifted one by KL with a stop-gradient anchor, weight 0.001. About twice the training cost. Plus feature-level equivariance: FME (a sinusoidal embedding of the raw pitch/onset values with a learnable bias and a linear layer, so a shift of the input is a fixed rotation of each sin/cos pair; seen in the code) and multi-dimensional relative attention (MRA) in place of RoPE.
- **Ablation (Table 1, PDF).** NTP loss / equivariance loss / Top-1 match: no regulariser 0.912 / 0.286 / 0.820; FME+MRA only 0.889 / 0.290 / 0.709 (**no equivariance gain, Top-1 worse**); data augmentation + FME+MRA 0.853 / 0.069 / 0.901; equivariance loss only 0.849 / 0.077 / 0.901; **EMT (both) 0.847 / 0.049 / 0.917**. The equivariance loss also lowers the next-token loss (0.912 -> 0.849).
- **Listening (Table 2).** Rating drop from unshifted to shifted prompts: EMT 3.18 -> 2.98 (0.20), Anticipatory M 2.68 -> 2.10 (0.58); Wilcoxon p = 0.039 / 0.008 on the two comparisons shown. A small test.
- **Not in the paper:** any conditioning on melody, chords or audio; electronic or goa material; limitations section.

## FOR US — what could go along with the melody work
1. **A no-training probe: is our melody control transposition-equivariant?** Steer with a target shifted by k semitones (the `same_chroma` head, or `target_raw`), transcribe the output (MuScriptor) and test whether its chroma moves by k (a circular roll). Their Top-1 / Jaccard idea is the metric shape. Cheap (renders only), fits E13 and the D15/D16 trajectory work. Registered as E21.
2. **If the probe says no:** their regulariser transfers to the small heads (5-10M parameters, so the doubled cost is small). It needs paired latents of the same audio pitch-shifted (bungee, never sox), and the head's output on the shifted pair should be the rolled output on the original. Their own result says augmentation alone gets most of the gain (0.069 against 0.049), so try augmentation first.
3. **Features alone do nothing** (FME+MRA row): do not expect a new embedding to fix equivariance in a conditioner; the training signal does.
4. **A symbolic Stage-1 generator** (Kim's two-stage idea: melody trajectory first, audio second): the released model is a prompt-continuation generator trained on general Lakh MIDI. Domain gap to goa and acid melodies, and our contour alphabet (L3, 15 symbols) would need a converter; evaluate before building anything.
5. **Our Head-B contour conditioner is transposition-invariant by construction** (relative contour), so the probe in item 1 does not apply to it; absolute register is its unconstrained part.
- **Does not apply:** anything on the audio DiT itself (their transforms act on symbolic input); the doubling of training cost on the large models.
