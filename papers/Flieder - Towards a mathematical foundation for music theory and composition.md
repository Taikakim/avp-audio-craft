# Towards a mathematical foundation for music theory and composition: a theory of structure

**Drew Flieder** (UC Santa Barbara, Dept. of Music) · *Journal of Mathematics and Music*, 2024
DOI 10.1080/17459737.2024.2379788 · read 2026-08-12 (WINTERMUTE), pp.1–3

**Verdict: no control-target yield. Foundations/ontology, filed short.**

---

## What it contains

Proposed **mathematical foundations** for music theory, grounded in **topos theory** —
categories, presheaves, ontology. It extends **Mazzola's** *The Topos of Music* (2002) to a
wider class of musical structures, and is explicitly *meta-theoretical*: a systematic method for
concept formation, not a theory of any particular musical phenomenon.

Three stated design criteria: **comprehensive comprehension** (accommodate a broad class of
musical objects), **explicit encoding** (capture their essential features), **minimal
ontological commitments** (posit as few primitives as possible).

Motivation, in his framing: mathematical music theory itself often "lacks a solid foundation in
mathematics" — pitch-class set theory relies on intuitive notions and advanced constructions get
assembled ad hoc. A unified ontology would let e.g. Babbitt and Gagaku be theorised without
altering the foundation.

Application examples (§5, illustrating generality rather than advancing any area): ordered sets
and lists; groups and twelve-tone theory; Mazzola's gesture theory; modules and score objects;
**parameter spaces for synthesizers**.

## What stays ours

**Nothing, as a control target — and I want to be exact about why, because my prediction was
wrong in an instructive way.** I expected this to be more harmonic theory, and therefore
over-powered for goa in the way C's Rohrmeier read established. It is *not* harmonic theory at
all: it is foundational ontology, a level above. The prediction was wrong; the verdict —
low yield for us — happens to hold anyway, for a different reason. Worth noting because "I
guessed the conclusion correctly" is not the same as "my reasoning was sound", and only reading
it distinguishes the two.

Against Kim's three-part test it fails all three legs: nothing computable from a clip, nothing
askable by ear, nothing that is a time series. It supplies no quantity.

**The one item I would keep**, and it is thin: the application list includes **parameter spaces
for synthesizers**. A foundational framework that treats synth parameter spaces as one of its
worked objects is at least gesturing at the territory our control work occupies — controllable
parameters as mathematical objects with structure. If anyone ever wants a *principled* account
of what a "control dimension" is, as opposed to our current operational one (a scalar we can
measure and steer), this literature is where that account would live. That is a someday-question
about foundations, not a this-quarter question about heads.

There is also an **Appendix 2** where the author contextualises the framework informally and
describes a composition built on it — the practice-facing part, unread.

## Status

Read pp.1–3 (abstract, introduction, foundational principles, roadmap). **The theory itself
(§§4–6: the presheaf construction, the formal definition of structure, the denotator
translation problem) and both appendices are unread.** Filed at this depth deliberately: the
question I needed answered — does this name anything we could measure or steer — is settled by
the introduction and the application list, and reading 40 more pages of topos theory would not
change it.
