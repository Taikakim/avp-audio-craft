# Week 2026-W39 synopsis (Sep 21 – Sep 27, entries run Sep 21–27)
*55 entries · an externally-built modular optimizer was audited and found mostly inert, a NaN guard that certified divergence as health became a named bug class, and a misdiagnosed GPU "model failure" turned out to be a render-path fault*

The week opened with an untracked-files sweep that surfaced a large uncommitted body of work: a new modular optimizer, a variance-dampening latent barrier loss and a standalone LoRA trainer, built in a parallel effort by the external agent ANTIGRAVITY rather than by the fleet. GHOST-NOTE cross-checked its core finding (a corrupted clip's generated-latent variance exceeding its checkpoint's normal range causes decoder-side single-sample discontinuities) against an independent corruption investigation, matched one z0 std value bit-for-bit, and reproduced the inference-time fix (clamp the latent std before decode) with a 99%+ reduction in bad samples on a clip from its own corpus. The work was committed as plain commits under the tree's default identity, not a fleet handle. A small provenance mix-up followed: three live-looking files were read by THE-FINN, GHOST-NOTE and WINTERMUTE as launch prep by the external thread, and CONTINUITY then corrected that they were its own mid-audit edits. WINTERMUTE's retrospective was that file mtimes give timing, not authorship, and that three instances converging on a wrong story in four minutes was not evidence. CONTINUITY's audit itself was the week's main technical thread: its loss guard tested a bare `mean_loss > threshold`, which is False for NaN, so a diverged epoch was logged as "healthy" until the GPU aborted two epochs later; and a run launched with escape-velocity and two variance-dampening flags had all three mechanisms inert for its whole length (the escape-velocity multiplier provably always 1.0 while costing GPU syncs, the barrier hinge reading 0.000 every step, the running latent std of 0.92–1.12 never reaching the 1.20 threshold). A schedule-free iterate swap was also silently skipped for the new optimizer, so demos and checkpoints rendered the training iterate rather than the averaged one. WINTERMUTE recognised the same dynamically-inert shape in an earlier LatCH head result and queued a roughly 30-line default-on inertness telemetry fix, not yet implemented. Mid-week the fleet absorbed a data-drive reshuffle onto a new NVMe (with checksum verification before any deletion, then a broken-symlink sweep that repaired 2352 links the move itself had broken), ran the overdue weekly routine (including a duplicate-fork near-miss, since a "failed" fork had in fact written its files), and then turned to a DoRA problem: a shampoo run's step-6340 "crash" proved to be the demo render, not the model. Repeated live-adapter renders in one process returned NaN or about 1e11 on identical inputs; merged adapters and the base model were deterministic. No local fix was found by the end of the week. The week closed with the resumed run finishing, and an audition that favoured the melody-subspace loss.

## Headlines
- A NaN-permissive `x > threshold` loss guard (a bug class, not an incident) logged "Loss healthy (nan <= 1.0)" and let training run two more epochs until a GPU abort (HSA_STATUS_ERROR_EXCEPTION). Fixed with an explicit finiteness check. The data was ruled out: all 300 latents in the subset scanned clean (max abs 10.73). The HSA fault itself was not root-caused.
- A run with modular escape-velocity and two variance-dampening flags had all three mechanisms inert throughout (hinge 0.000 every logged step; running latent std 0.92–1.12 vs a 1.20 threshold; escape-velocity multiplier exactly 1.0 forever). Conclusions the run produced about those mechanisms are therefore unsupported. The audit now has two halves: statically unreachable flags, and dynamically inert components.
- Schedule-free swap hooks were gated on one optimizer class, so the new optimizer was excluded and every schedule-free demo and checkpoint saved the training iterate, not the averaged one. Fixed.
- WINTERMUTE recognised an earlier "inert" LatCH rms_energy_air head as the same family: the target equalled the head's own mean (about a 0.26-sigma request) with gradient norms around 1e-5 against |x| around 400. A default-on, single-sync inertness check was queued, not implemented that week.
- Tree sweep of five repos left all in sync; one repo's dirty list fell from 45 entries to 5 by moving about 7.5 GB of untracked artefacts into per-clone excludes (thin-fork convention, nothing deleted). 2.9 GB of render directories remain in the tree and need relocating.
- A single orphaned documentation diff documented that `load_lora` on a live model cannot swap to a different LoRA rank (size mismatch on lora_A/lora_B); the fix is a fresh model load per checkpoint when rank can change. Committed.
- Drive layout changed: a new 256 GB NVMe took the timeseries sidecars and several latent sets; about 24 duplicate latent directories (about 82 GB) were deleted from the OS drive only after per-file checksum comparison. The follow-up sweep found 2352 symlinks broken by the move (all repointed) and about 43.8k pre-existing unrelated broken links. One earlier statement that a caption set stays on its old drive was corrected as superseded.
- A claim that avp latents carry embedded captions was corrected: the per-crop prompts are artist names only (3 distinct values over the items), and real tiered captions live in a separate sidecar file.
- LoRA gauge-drift check: B velocity matched B·A velocity to 3 digits, so trajectory velocity numbers reflect real function motion; the gauge share of step energy rose from 4.8% to 12.4% (chance 2%), judged small but worth watching.
- A LoRA-TSD optimizer was delivered with 71 CPU tests and two critic passes, but not run on audio and with GPU speed unmeasured. A P95 step-governor proposal was reviewed and not adopted. A raw-gradient flight recorder was added (on by default, never alters training).
- DoRA render fault (negative/unresolved): live-adapter renders give NaN or about 1e11 intermittently under the caching allocator; caching off is bit-exact; merged adapters are deterministic. New firmware did not fix it (6/6 live runs failed), nor did ROCm 10.1 (3/3). Every crash is a GPU instruction-fetch fault. A linux-lts boot test was the next local step; a cluster cross-check was off the table because the project's cluster GPU allocation was used up.
- Shampoo run resumed after three resume bugs, the silent one being an unsaved optimizer step counter that restarted warmup and flipped schedule-free averaging from active to inert (caught by the step-6500 audit; relaunch stopped before saving). Finished at step 10144 with zero non-finite gradient steps; merged re-renders at 7608/8876/10144 were 18/18 finite, 10144 best (5/6 clean). The flight recorder logged 5 lone spikes (up to 42.7x rolling median), all in conditioning-input adapters.
- A correction was posted: the r256 run's "NaN at step 6340" was the render fault (merged re-render 24/24 finite); its real failure is a training NaN near step 6900. The rule "a demo NaN is an early warning of the weights" was withdrawn. Both step-6340 grids were rendered, scored and published (216 cells), and a scoring-scope bug that let new cells pass the latent-sanity gate unchecked was fixed.
- D18 was found trained to completion on Sep 10 and stable (max gnorm 0.268), but never rendered, so its adherence question remains unanswered. D17 trained to completion, with the contour A/B never run. Several cluster-job entries in the experiments file could not be verified.
- Listening pass (Sep 27): the melody-subspace-loss arms were the most melodic heard, at some cost to kick and bass; policy recorded to keep it (K=5). Older schedule-free, Lion and fusion trainings beat the newer modular ones at the goa style, partly because "epoch" is not comparable across sequence lengths (about 21x more latent frames in one case) and partly a hidden roughly 0.2x learning-rate effect of the SNR gate.
