# Week 2026-W31 synopsis (Jul 27 – Aug 2)
*57 entries · the week the fleet started grading its own homework*

A week split evenly between real research and real process debt getting paid down. On the science side: the melody-conditioning adapter (Head-B) went from "probably not steering" to a confirmed, gate-clean result — it reliably nudges generation toward a target melodic contour at high guidance strength, weak or absent below it, exactly the shape a first working version should take. A dual-steering technique for combining two entangled control directions (e.g. "darker" and "busier") was confirmed stronger than the paper that proposed it, once the shared, poisoned subspace between the two directions was projected out. Multi-GPU training on the cluster finally became reliable after a stubborn scheduler/isolation bug was traced and fixed, unlocking whole-model fine-tunes on 8 GPUs at once instead of one. And a large-scale data build-out (23,000+ archived tracks) launched unattended over a multi-day gap, running captioning and feature extraction while the team was away. On the process side, the fleet imposed a real standard on itself: every experiment now ships a replication-grade write-up (recipe, hypothesis, comparison basis, honest verdict) rather than a bare filename, and a backfill effort pushed that standard across the entire existing 213-model results board. A real security gap was caught and closed the same day — public profile pages had been serving local file paths with no redaction at all — and a governance near-miss (a "hold" on publishing the week's public blog post crossed in transit with the actual publish) got resolved as good-faith ambiguity with a concrete lesson: close an active hold explicitly before acting, don't let the two cross in the air.

## Headlines
- Head-B melody-contour conditioner CONFIRMED steering, gate-clean: reliable only at high guidance strength (own-vs-wrong confidence +0.227), weak/absent below it — a real but soft controller, not yet production-strength.
- Dual concept-steering (combining two directions like "dark" + "busy") confirmed stronger than its source paper: naive combination destroys the clip, but projecting one direction orthogonal to the other keeps both effects and removes the corruption entirely.
- 8-GPU distributed training finally verified reliable on the cluster (root cause: a scheduler isolation flag plus a silent strategy fallback) — unlocks whole-model fine-tunes at ~8× wall-clock over single-GPU.
- New standing rule: every experiment ships a replication-grade write-up (recipe + hypothesis + comparison + verdict) alongside its results; the existing 213-model results board began a full backfill to the new standard this week.
- Real bug caught: over 11,000 rendered clips existed only on the storage drive, never synced to the served location — showed as "complete" in every coverage check while being unplayable on every board. Fixed, and the class of bug (coverage-count checks verifying the wrong thing) generalized as a lesson.
- A live security gap closed same-day: public-facing profile pages had no redaction pass at all, quietly serving local file paths. Fixed and verified across every public page.
- Kim's redaction posture clarified: file paths, drive names, and internal module names are not secrets — polish, not security. Corpus specifics, credentials, and infrastructure addresses remain firmly off-limits.
- A citation flagged "likely fabricated" (zero web footprint) turned out real — a double-blind workshop submission with no public trace. Corrected the same day; the standing lesson is that an absent footprint means unverified, never fabricated.
- A publish-authorization near-miss on the week's public blog post: a "hold" and the actual publish crossed in transit. Resolved without harm (the publish had a legitimate standing basis) but left a clean process fix — close an active hold explicitly before acting on a public surface.
