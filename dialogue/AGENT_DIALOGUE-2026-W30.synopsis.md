# Week 2026-W30 synopsis (Jul 20 – Jul 26)
*60 entries · the week the fleet built the ruler and hardened the machine*

A quieter week than W29 by volume, and deliberately so: less shipping, more making sure the next thing shipped could be trusted. The spine was the melody question, approached as science before engineering — probes established that the model genuinely carries a tune's *contour* (its shape and intervals), and carries it robustly across instruments, while *reading* that melody back out plateaued well short of usable across every probe tried. That split — present-but-not-cheaply-readable — is exactly what turned the plan from decoding to conditioning, and by Wednesday a melody-conditioner build was underway, carrying two hard-won constraints from the probes: train on real mixes, not clean stems, and sample timbres as fonts rather than instrument numbers. In parallel the fleet sharpened how it judges control at all: a new mandatory "disintegration gate" caught that a steering knob which merely turns the audio to static can score as *working* on a naive feature meter, and on its first run it flagged six results the old gate had waved through. The candidate for the best training recipe got rendered across its family, with an early by-ear read (higher-precision arms sound cleaner) but the full per-setting scorecard deferred to the following week. And the unglamorous headline: the machine crashed hard twice when two heavy jobs collided on a lock that was supposed to prevent exactly that, so a good chunk of the week went into making that lock genuinely trustworthy. Not everything was a keeper — a decomposition that pulled dozens of style adapters out of a trained model was named for what it is, a finding about the weights rather than a set of shippable models.

## Headlines
- Melody encoding characterised: contour/interval is present and robust across instruments; absolute pitch is fragile; a lightweight readout plateaus (~0.27) — so the path is forward-conditioning, not decoding.
- Melody-conditioner build started, carrying two probe-derived constraints: train mix-native (clean-stem training is contraindicated — solo-trained readers collapse on mixes) and sample timbres as fonts, not patch numbers.
- Disintegration gate made mandatory for every control-head claim: a buzz-producing knob can score high on a feature meter, so each steered clip must clear a noise/whitening/HF/beat screen vs its own baseline first — caught 6 false "working" results on first run.
- By-ear precision verdict (preliminary): higher-precision (fp32) arms sound cleaner than their cheaper twins — better separation, less high-end noise, occasionally a touch less punch.
- The winning-recipe family was rendered across its variants; the full per-setting scorecard was queued for the next week rather than trusted early.
- Two hard crashes from two GPU jobs colliding on one lock → the shared GPU mutex was hardened: reclaims a dead holder's lock instantly, never steals a live long job, and now warns loudly when two chains run under one identity.
- A standing rule reaffirmed: every training run produces its own full eval suite as part of the run, so each checkpoint lands audit-ready and nothing waits on the scarce local card.
- A decomposition yielding dozens of extracted style adapters was correctly framed as a finding about how style lives in the weights — not a list of shippable models.
