Overcoming the Loop Attractor: Constrained Sampling, Operator Theory, and Latent Sequence Rectification in Continuous Generative Flows
1. The Architectural Paradigm and the Loop Attractor
The transition from discrete, token-based autoregression to continuous-state flow matching and diffusion models represents a profound evolution in generative sequence modeling. Current state-of-the-art systems, such as the SA3 architecture, utilize rectified flows or diffusion processes to map a prior Gaussian distribution to a complex data manifold through a learned vector field. Unlike Large Language Models (LLMs) that rely on a discrete vocabulary and a discrete key-value (KV) cache, continuous flow architectures operate on a continuous compressed latent sequence1. In high-fidelity audio contexts, this state space often consists of a heavily compressed latent representation—for instance, 256 variational autoencoder (VAE) channels operating at a frame rate of 10.767 Hz, where a single sequence crop of    encompasses over six minutes of audio data1. While these architectures excel at modeling local distributional statistics, including genre features, gross timbre, and localized rhythmic coherence, their extension to unbounded, infinite-horizon generation reliably triggers a catastrophic failure mode known as the loop attractor.
The loop attractor manifests when the generative sequence extends beyond the strict constraints of its initial conditioning or the finite temporal window seen during training. In unconstrained regions, the model's only contextual anchor is its own emerging, self-generated output. Because the flow-matching training objective is fundamentally local—optimizing step-wise denoising or velocity field prediction without penalizing macroscopic structural decay—the model lacks any gradient pressure regarding long-range temporal statistics, such as the recurrence spectrum or novelty rate1. Consequently, the maximum-likelihood behavior is to repeat the most recently established coherent phrase, creating a self-feeding limit cycle. The resulting sequence becomes trapped in a low-entropy attractor of the autoregressive map, maintaining local acoustic or visual coherence while entirely losing macro-structural evolution1.
This pathology is compounded by the "posterior-mean collapse" of unconstrained features. Empirical noise-invariance sweeps indicate that while rhythmic grids survive high noise injections, melodic and harmonic trajectories rapidly degrade to the corpus mean1. As these features collapse into generic filler, the repetition prior freezes them into a static, repeating spectral image. Thus, the loop attractor is not a bug in the numerical solver, but rather the mathematically faithful behavior of a prior that was never explicitly trained to recognize or generate long-range novelty. The resolution of this defect requires supplying the missing long-range structural signal through sophisticated interventions at inference time, modifications to the training regime, or fundamental shifts in the operator-theoretic representation of the generative dynamics.
2. Geometric Collapse and Exposure Bias
Understanding the loop attractor necessitates a formal examination of the underlying geometric and statistical mechanisms that drive generative drift. Two primary forces interact to degrade continuous sequence generation over extended horizons: geometric collapse within the model's internal representation space, and the compounding distributional mismatch known as exposure bias.
2.1 Geometric Collapse and Reinforced Mode Regulation
From a dynamical-systems perspective, the autoregressive generation of a sequence can be viewed as the iteration of a stochastic map. Ideally, the invariant measure of this map should exhibit mixing properties consistent with the entropy rate of the target data manifold, ensuring sustained diversity1. However, empirical analyses of long-form generation reveal that trajectories frequently suffer from geometric collapse, wherein the internal state space of the model becomes confined to an abnormally low-dimensional sub-manifold2.
This phenomenon can be quantified using the correlation dimension, a fractal-geometric measure of self-similarity that captures the epistemological complexity of the latent trajectory4. As a model enters a loop attractor, its correlation dimension drops precipitously, reflecting a loss of state-space accessibility. In transformer-based architectures, this collapse is often localized within the value cache, which acts as a temporal echo chamber that reinforces dominant, persistent directions until the output becomes entirely periodic2.
To counteract this, researchers have proposed inference-time interventions such as Reinforced Mode Regulation (RMR). RMR serves as a lightweight, online state-space regulator that identifies and applies low-rank damping to the dominant self-reinforcing eigenvectors within the value cache5. By explicitly dampening the eigenvalues associated with repetitive modes, RMR artificially preserves the dimensionality of the state space, allowing the generation to proceed stably at drastically lower entropy rates without collapsing into limit cycles2. This demonstrates that the loop attractor is an inherently geometric defect of the continuous latent state, addressable via targeted topological regulation.
2.2 Exposure Bias and the Compounding of Error
Operating in tandem with geometric collapse is the classical problem of exposure bias. Standard training paradigms, often referred to as Teacher Forcing, supervise the model by conditioning it exclusively on clean, ground-truth historical context7. During inference, however, the model must rely on its own imperfect, self-generated context. In continuous diffusion and flow matching, the resulting distributional shift pushes the iterative generation progressively off the learned data manifold, leading to macroscopic drift characterized by semantic freezing, color saturation in video, or pad-filling in audio7.

3. Directional Rectification and Frequency Compensation
Recent theoretical advances suggest that exposure bias should not merely be viewed as an error to be minimized, but as a rich, multi-dimensional signal that contains the necessary information for its own correction. The Directional-Frequency Adaptive Rectification (DEFAR) framework operationalizes this insight by utilizing the bias itself as a dynamic feedback mechanism during the training of flow-matching models10.
3.1 Anti-Drift Rectification
The first component of this framework, Anti-Drift Rectification (ADR), addresses the spatial directionality of exposure bias. Traditional alignment or regularization methods typically rely on static constraints that fail to adapt to the specific trajectory of the generated sample. ADR, by contrast, simulates a single-step inference process during the training phase to explicitly measure the inference-time drift—the exact vector deviation between the self-generated state and the target ground-truth trajectory11.
By treating this drift as a supervisory signal, ADR constructs a dynamic regularizer that explicitly learns the vector direction required to steer the deviated state back toward the target distribution. This endows the flow-matching model with an intrinsic, active self-rectification capability, fundamentally altering the learned vector field to include a restorative force that pulls off-manifold trajectories back to safety10.
3.2 Frequency Compensation
The second component of the DEFAR framework addresses a distinct spectral vulnerability identified in continuous flow models: the disproportionate loss of low-frequency components during the early, high-noise stages of generative denoising10. In hierarchical generation, the early high-noise steps are responsible for establishing the global macro-structure (low frequencies) of the sequence, while later steps decorate this structure with high-frequency detail. Exposure bias uniquely disrupts this process, resulting in an output that severely lacks structural anchoring.
Empirical frequency analysis reveals that the accumulated bias is heavily concentrated in these missing low-frequency regions11. Frequency Compensation (FC) directly exploits this by utilizing the magnitude of the exposure bias as a self-feedback weighting factor. Applied as a modification to the original training objective, FC creates a negative feedback loop that dynamically reinforces the learning of the missing low-frequency structural components11. By forcing the network to prioritize the broad structural strokes before focusing on high-frequency noise, the DEFAR framework prevents the structural decay that ultimately leads to repetitive looping. Extensive benchmarking on complex datasets demonstrates that combining ADR and FC endows models with superior inference robustness, architectural compatibility, and resistance to long-horizon deterioration11.
4. Inference-Time Steering via Feynman-Kac Frameworks
For scenarios where altering the base model parameters through retraining is computationally prohibitive or undesirable, inference-time steering provides a mathematically rigorous alternative. This approach frames the generation process as sampling from a target distribution that has been explicitly tilted by an auxiliary energy potential.

Sampling from this unnormalized target distribution is non-trivial, particularly in high-dimensional continuous state spaces, necessitating the use of advanced Sequential Monte Carlo (SMC) techniques.
4.2 Feynman-Kac Interacting Particle Systems
Feynman-Kac (FK) steering operationalizes this tilted sampling by deploying a system of multiple interacting diffusion processes, referred to as particles14. As the generative model simulates the reverse diffusion process or integrates the flow ODE, the FK-IPS framework evaluates each particle against the potential function    at intermediate timesteps14.
The core mechanism of FK steering is iterative resampling. Particles that exhibit high potential scores—indicating trajectories that successfully minimize recurrence and maximize desired structural properties—are duplicated, while particles with low scores are terminated14. This evolutionary approach guarantees asymptotic convergence to the exact tilted distribution, enabling the generation of rare, high-reward sequences that the base model would never naturally produce15. FK steering operates entirely as a post-hoc intervention, requiring zero gradient updates to the model weights, making it universally applicable across discrete and continuous-state diffusion architectures14.
4.3 Feynman-Kac-Flow for Deterministic Models
While FK steering is native to stochastic differential equations, recent breakthroughs have successfully extended the framework to Conditional Flow Matching (CFM). The Feynman-Kac-Flow (FK-Flow) methodology reformulates the deterministic probability paths of flow matching using the Feynman-Kac formula, thereby allowing the injection of energy potentials into purely deterministic vector fields13.
In FK-Flow, the accumulation of the potential is tracked alongside the ODE integration. To prevent the collapse of all particles into a single deterministic path (a unique challenge not present in stochastic diffusion), FK-Flow strategically injects trace amounts of Brownian motion—effectively converting the deterministic flow into a controlled stochastic process—prior to evaluating the resampling weights18. This technique has been rigorously validated in complex physical domains, such as the generation of chemical transition states where the model must navigate tight geometric constraints related to molecular chirality13. By penalizing invalid spatial configurations through the energy potential, FK-Flow steers the deterministic model toward physically accurate geometries13. By analogy, when applied to latent sequence generation, the potential function evaluates the recurrence matrix of the audio or video crop, systematically terminating branches that exhibit limit-cycle behavior1.
4.4 Resolving Weight Degeneracy via TRI-TSMC
A critical limitation of standard FK steering in high dimensions is weight degeneracy. Because the base model proposes transitions blindly with respect to the terminal reward, the majority of particles will naturally drift into low-reward regions, causing their importance weights to vanish20. This requires simulating an impractical number of particles to maintain a diverse and accurate ensemble.
Twisted SMC addresses this inefficiency by introducing a look-ahead twisting function that pre-conditions the proposal distribution, steering particles toward high-reward regions before the resampling step even occurs20. Because the optimal twisting function is computationally intractable, Trust-Region Iterative Twisted Sequential Monte Carlo (TRI-TSMC) provides a tractable approximation. TRI-TSMC formulates the learning of the twisting function as an iterative policy update within a trust region20. At each step, it computes an exact Kullback-Leibler constrained update in path space using tempered importance reweighting, then projects this target back to a parameterized family via weighted maximum likelihood20. This process drastically reduces the variance of the importance weights, enabling highly efficient, zero-variance sampling with minimal particle budgets, thereby making real-time tilted generation computationally feasible20.
5. Training-Time Interventions: Diffusion Forcing and History Guidance
When continuous-time inference interventions are deemed too computationally intensive, the structural priors required for long-horizon stability must be embedded directly into the model's parameters during training. Diffusion Forcing represents a fundamental reconceptualization of how sequence models ingest and process temporal uncertainty.
5.1 The Mechanics of Diffusion Forcing
Traditional autoregressive models condition purely on clean historical data, while standard diffusion models denoise an entire static sequence uniformly. Diffusion Forcing synthesizes these paradigms by training a causal sequence model to generate future states while conditioning on a history that has been subjected to heterogeneous, independent per-frame noise levels23.
By treating noise injection as a continuous, fractional analog to discrete token masking, Diffusion Forcing forces the network to learn robust denoising rules under varying degrees of contextual certainty7. During training, the model observes completely clean frames (acting as absolute context), partially corrupted frames (simulating the imperfect outputs of an autoregressive rollout), and completely noised frames (representing the generation target). This diverse exposure immunizes the model against the compounding drift of exposure bias, as the network is explicitly trained to recover the true data manifold even when conditioned on highly degraded historical inputs24.
5.2 Streaming Causal Generation and DiscoForcing
The flexibility of independent noise scheduling proves exceptionally powerful in real-time, streaming environments characterized by non-stationary conditioning signals. The DiscoForcing architecture adapts Diffusion Forcing to the challenge of streaming audio-driven generation, where a model must produce coherent continuous sequences (such as full-body motion) that respond instantly to abrupt changes in the input audio—such as tempo shifts, beat drops, or sudden silence—without revising past outputs26.
DiscoForcing achieves this strict causal responsiveness through a hybrid temporal noise schedule comprising random, monotonic, and trapezoidal noise profiles26. This training regime uniquely equips the model for "history-guided streaming sampling." When the incoming audio conditioning suddenly conflicts with the established historical trajectory, the sampler dynamically applies higher noise levels to the stale historical latents, effectively discounting them26. This forces the generation to decouple from the outdated context and adhere strictly to the new audio evidence, preventing the model from becoming anchored to obsolete motion or acoustic patterns26.
5.3 Elucidated Rolling Diffusion and Uncertainty Growth
To explicitly model the progressive growth of predictive uncertainty inherent in chaotic dynamical systems, researchers have developed Rolling Diffusion frameworks. Elucidated Rolling Diffusion Models (ERDM) combine the performant preconditioning of state-of-the-art diffusion architectures with snapshot-dependent noise schedules that monotonically escalate noise for distant future snapshots28.
By applying progressively larger noise levels to later timesteps, ERDM mirrors the natural expansion of uncertainty over time28. This sliding window denoising process ensures that local temporal structures are solidified before distant futures are finalized, providing a robust mathematical structure for complex spatiotemporal forecasting and long-sequence extrapolation without succumbing to immediate geometric collapse28.
5.4 Multidimensional History Guidance
The capacity of Diffusion Forcing to model the joint distribution of all possible sub-sequences unlocks novel, inference-only control mechanisms known collectively as History Guidance30. By treating history not as a static input but as a flexible conditioning variable that can be partially masked by noise, users can exert fine-grained control over the generative rollout.
Temporal History Guidance allows for the composition of long-horizon semantic stability with local reactive dynamics, ensuring that the sequence respects both macroscopic and microscopic constraints30. More critically for the prevention of limit cycles, Fractional History Guidance applies noise selectively to the high-frequency components of the historical context while preserving the low-frequency structural data30. By intentionally unconstraining the high-frequency textural details, Fractional History Guidance forces the model to constantly synthesize new variations, explicitly preventing the output from freezing into a static, repetitive loop, thereby maintaining dynamic sequence evolution over extended horizons30.
6. On-Policy Refinement: Self-Forcing and Amortized Correctors
While Diffusion Forcing improves robustness to imperfect context, it still trains the model on data derived from the true ground-truth distribution. To completely eradicate the train-test discrepancy, the training regime must simulate the exact conditions of inference, a paradigm formalized as Self-Forcing.
6.1 Autoregressive Unrolling and DMD Distillation
Self-Forcing explicitly addresses exposure bias by conditioning the generation of frame    entirely on the model's own previously generated frames,   , thereby performing a true autoregressive rollout during the training loop8. Because generating a full diffusion chain for every step of a training rollout is computationally intractable, practical Self-Forcing relies on high-speed distilled student models, typically utilizing Distribution Matching Distillation (DMD) to reduce the generation process to just a few integration steps8.
A central innovation enabling this technique is the integration of key-value (KV) caching into the training process, a feature traditionally reserved for inference7. Furthermore, to maintain a manageable memory footprint during backpropagation, Self-Forcing employs stochastic gradient truncation, choosing only a single random denoising step per frame to pass gradients through8. This allows the model to be supervised by a holistic, sequence-level loss function that evaluates the distribution of the entire generated video or audio track, rather than relying on isolated frame-wise reconstruction errors25. By explicitly forcing the model to navigate and recover from its own compounding errors, Self-Forcing extends the coherent generation horizon from seconds to minutes, drastically suppressing drift and limit-cycle collapse7.

	6.2 Amortized Guidance via Actor-Critic RL
For deployments where retraining the massive foundational prior is impossible, and where the computational cost of simulating multiple SMC particles is prohibitive, Amortized Inpainting with Diffusion (AID) offers an optimal compromise32. AID freezes the pre-trained continuous diffusion backbone entirely and trains a lightweight, reusable guidance module offline, which is then deployed across arbitrary generative tasks without requiring per-instance optimization32.
The theoretical elegance of AID lies in its formulation of sequence correction as a deterministic optimal control problem subject to a supervised terminal objective33. Because solving deterministic optimal control directly in high-dimensional state spaces is highly unstable, AID derives an auxiliary randomized Gaussian formulation. A rigorous mathematical proof establishes an exact, optimizer-preserving bridge: finding the optimal policy for the randomized Gaussian control problem analytically recovers the optimal deterministic guidance field through the policy's mean32.
This bridge translates the guidance problem into a standard continuous-time reinforcement learning (RL) framework33. Employing a model-free Actor-Critic algorithm, the critic evaluates the integrated   -function over the state distribution, while the actor updates the policy gradient to minimize the divergence from the target structure34. The resulting amortized guidance module operates with minimal trainable overhead—adding less than one percent to the parameter count—while significantly elevating the speed-quality frontier by applying immediate, deterministic velocity corrections that steer the generation safely away from geometric collapse32.
7. Operator-Theoretic Paradigms: Linearizing the Generative Flow
All previously discussed methods attempt to guide, constrain, or regularize the highly non-linear vector fields inherent to continuous diffusion and flow matching. A more fundamental architectural evolution involves operator theory, which posits that non-linear dynamical systems can be perfectly modeled as linear systems if mapped into an appropriate, highly dimensional embedding space.
7.1 Koopman Operator Theory and Offline Distillation
Koopman operator theory provides the mathematical foundation for this shift. It states that the evolution of any non-linear dynamical system can be described by an infinite-dimensional linear operator (the Koopman operator) acting on a space of observable functions37. For continuous generative models, this implies that the iterative, non-linear denoising trajectory can be completely linearized39.
The Koopman Distillation Model (KDM) exploits this property for offline distillation. An encoder network maps the highly noised input state    into an embedded latent space. Within this space, a learned, finite-dimensional matrix approximation of the Koopman operator,   , evolves the state forward in a single mathematical step:   38. A decoder then reconstructs the clean sequence from this evolved embedding. Theoretical proofs within the KDM framework confirm that, under mild assumptions, diffusion dynamics admit a precise finite-dimensional Koopman representation, and that proximity in this Koopman space strictly correlates with semantic and structural similarity in the data39. By reducing generation to linear matrix multiplication in a latent space, KDM bypasses the step-by-step error accumulation that feeds the loop attractor.
7.2 Spectral Decoupling via KoopmanFlow
The principles of Koopman theory have also been utilized to solve the representational entanglement that plagues continuous generative policies. In sequence generation, slow, global structural shifts (low frequencies) are often computationally entangled with fast, reactive local textures (high frequencies). KoopmanFlow addresses this by bifurcating the generative architecture37.
Operating within a unified latent space, KoopmanFlow routes low-frequency, macro-level generation through a Koopman reconstruction branch that collapses the entire structural trajectory into a single step (NFE=1)37. Concurrently, a localized, time-variant branch utilizes standard flow matching to isolate and generate high-frequency residuals stimulated by environmental cues37. By structurally decoupling the linear, time-invariant global inertia from the highly stochastic, non-linear local texture, KoopmanFlow prevents the high-frequency dynamics from destabilizing the global structure, providing robust immunity against geometric collapse in continuous sequences.
7.3 Spectral Mean Flows and Tensor Product Hilbert Spaces
Approaching sequence generation from a purely probabilistic operator perspective, Spectral Mean Flows offer a framework that avoids modeling stochastic recurrence entirely. Instead of generating a sequence step-by-step, this method embeds the distribution of the entire sequence as a single mathematical object—a tensor within a product Reproducing Kernel Hilbert Space (RKHS)41.
The generative process is then defined as a Maximum Mean Discrepancy (MMD) gradient flow operating directly on the space of sequence distributions41. To handle the immense dimensionality of tensor products, Spectral Mean Flows employ spectral decomposition of the linear operators underlying a Hidden Markov Model (HMM), deriving a highly scalable tensor network factorization41. Because the MMD flow aligns exactly with the continuity equation of flow matching, this approach enables end-to-end simulation-free learning41. By optimizing the sequence mean embedding holistically rather than autoregressively, Spectral Mean Flows are structurally immune to the localized feedback loops that create the loop attractor42.
7.4 State-Space Models as Continuous Sequence Priors
Complementing operator-theoretic advancements, modern State-Space Models (SSMs), particularly those based on the Mamba architecture, provide a highly efficient mechanism for enforcing long-range linear priors. SSMs process sequences linearly through selective scan mechanisms, offering theoretical unbounded context without the quadratic complexity of full attention45.
In complex spatial and temporal tasks, such as stereo image super-resolution or multi-view 3D generation, diffusion models often struggle to maintain global consistency. The integration of Disparity-Aware State-Space Modules (DASSM) or multi-view Mamba blocks directly into the diffusion backbone forces the network to respect a continuous, low-frequency structural prior across the entire sequence47. This hybrid approach—combining the localized, non-linear generative prowess of diffusion with the global, linear dependency modeling of SSMs—creates a sequence trajectory that inherently resists the high-frequency degeneration that leads to repetitive looping47.
8. Domain Specificity: Audio Latents and Structural Symmetries
The theoretical frameworks discussed above must be carefully adapted to the specific modalities of the continuous latent space. In high-fidelity audio generation—such as models operating on 10.767 Hz compressed representations—the structural symmetries of the data dictate the success of the generative prior1.
8.1 Exploiting Pitch Equivariance with CQT-Diff
Standard convolutional or transformer backbones applied to linear audio spectrograms fundamentally struggle to model music because they fail to respect harmonic relationships51. The Constant-Q Transform (CQT), which utilizes a logarithmically spaced frequency axis, is uniquely suited to audio because it translates pitch equivariance directly into spatial translation equivariance52.
The CQT-Diff framework demonstrates that preconditioning a neural diffusion model with an invertible CQT preserves these critical structural symmetries52. By utilizing a double real representation of the complex CQT features and strictly avoiding shift-based operations (such as bias terms in convolutional layers, which would disrupt delicate phase relationships), CQT-Diff maintains perfect synchrony between the real and imaginary components in the time-frequency space54.
This symmetry-preserving architecture allows CQT-Diff to act as a highly robust, universal generative prior. When applied to audio inverse problems such as bandwidth extension, declipping, or long-gap inpainting, the model utilizes posterior sampling strategies over the CQT latent space, dynamically guiding the generative trajectory using observation gradients without requiring any task-specific retraining53.
8.2 Practical Deployment for Latent Recurrence
For architectures like SA3 that suffer from extreme limit-cycle attractors during audio continuation, combining CQT-based structural priors with inference-time steering yields the most mathematically sound deployment strategy. Because the occurrence of a loop attractor can be reliably measured by a continuous, differentiable recurrence statistic—such as a whitened-patch novelty meter—this metric can be directly substituted as the energy potential    within a Feynman-Kac Flow or TRI-TSMC sampling framework1.
During the integration of the reverse flow, the SMC particles evaluate the structural recurrence of the evolving latent sequence at each step. Particles whose trajectories drift toward the loop attractor will exhibit spiking recurrence potentials, leading to their immediate termination during the resampling phase1. Conversely, particles exploring novel, non-repeating latent manifolds are amplified. This synthesis of domain-specific architectural symmetries (CQT) and rigorous optimal control (FK steering) provides a definitive, gradient-free mechanism to force continuous generative flows to extrapolate infinitely without collapsing into repetitive cycles.
9. Strategic Conclusions
The transition to continuous generative sequences has exposed fundamental flaws in localized, autoregressive likelihood training. The loop attractor, geometric collapse, and exposure bias are not independent bugs, but interconnected symptoms of a prior attempting to navigate an infinite horizon without a macroscopic map.
Resolving these issues demands a layered algorithmic strategy:
1. Immediate Inference Control: For deployed models, frameworks like Feynman-Kac-Flow and TRI-TSMC offer mathematically exact mechanisms to tilt the generative distribution away from limit cycles using differentiable recurrence potentials, albeit at a higher computational cost.
2. Amortized Efficiency: For real-time applications where particle simulation is too slow, Amortized Inpainting with Diffusion (AID) provides a continuous-time Actor-Critic RL solution, compiling optimal deterministic path corrections into a tiny, zero-shot guidance module.
3. Fundamental Prior Correction: When retraining is possible, moving from standard Teacher Forcing to Diffusion Forcing or Self-Forcing fundamentally rewires the model to handle uncertainty, while DEFAR directly harvests exposure bias to build intrinsic anti-drift vectors and reinforce low-frequency structural anchoring.
4. Operator-Theoretic Architectures: The future of long-horizon generation lies in linearizing the temporal flow. By embedding sequences into Koopman latent spaces or utilizing Spectral Mean Flows, models can bypass stochastic step-by-step recurrence entirely, ensuring that generative drift is structurally impossible.
By treating sequence continuation not merely as iterative denoising, but as the rigorous control of a continuous dynamical system, the vulnerability of the loop attractor can be systematically eliminated, unlocking the true potential of infinite-horizon generative models.
